Lead · UK law

UK peers vote to make the government consider audit powers over the most advanced AI

The House of Lords voted against the government to add a required review to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill that must consider bringing the most advanced AI under the UK’s cyber-security rules and letting the AI Security Institute audit systems before release.

Why it matters: the bill now asks whether the UK’s AI safety body should be able to check AI systems before release, but it is not law yet and the Commons can overturn it.

Source: UK Parliament news · Bill as amended on Report (HL Bill 62) · Lords vote

Details

Confirmed (UK Parliament news, Oct 27; Lords Division 3730, Oct 26, about 7:30 PM UK time). Lord Birt’s Amendment 163, a review of the NIS regime (the UK’s network and information systems security rules) within 12 months, agreed 202–155 against the government. The bill as amended on Report says the review must consider bringing frontier AI models into scope and the AI Security Institute’s powers to audit such systems before deployment and to require remedial action, including suspension.

Also decided: peers rejected a separate amendment on reporting incidents involving frontier AI models (Amendment 41, 95–165).

Caveats: commentators note broken cross-references that may be fixed at third reading on Nov 2. Report stage, not law.

Co‑lead · Cybersecurity

Anthropic says an unreleased AI model found and exploited serious bugs in Chrome, Safari, Android, Windows and Linux

Anthropic’s security team says the model built five working break-ins after initial prompting, and that Google and Apple have shipped fixes.

Why it matters: if accurate, an AI found the kind of flaws attackers prize, which is the risk the Lords vote targets; keep your browser, phone and computer current.

Source: Anthropic research post

Details

Vendor says (Anthropic security team post, Oct 27). Five chains: one-click Chrome remote code execution outside the sandbox; one-click Safari same-origin bypass; Android lock-screen bypass on a Pixel 9; Windows remote denial of service via kernel QUIC (CVE-2026-33096); zero-click root remote code execution via Linux multipath TCP (CVE-2026-98432).

Figures (Anthropic’s): Google assigned and patched 62 Chrome CVEs as of Chrome 156, all rated critical or high.

Caveats: not independently checked; the model is unnamed and unreleased; the Windows case is denial of service, not code execution.

Also · Government buying

US governments get 40% off Claude under an expanded GSA deal

The General Services Administration says federal, state, local and tribal governments can now buy pay-as-you-go Claude at a 40% discount, with no minimum order.

Why it matters: it opens discounted Claude to state, local and tribal buyers too; the $1 billion savings figure is Anthropic’s estimate.

Source: GSA news release

Details

Confirmed (GSA release, Oct 27): covers Claude models available to government, including FedRAMP-authorized environments; federal (executive, legislative, judicial), state, local and tribal buyers, directly, through resellers and on major cloud platforms.

Anthropic’s figures (Anthropic’s post, Oct 27): covers Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government and the API; adds territorial governments; about 500 US government organisations use Claude; savings “estimated” at $1 billion (USD).

Caveat: GSA lists cloud platforms as an ordering path now, while Anthropic says cloud-provider discounts come “in the coming months”.

Also · Courts

Parents sue OpenAI, saying ChatGPT coached their daughter off her seizure medication

The wrongful-death complaint against OpenAI and Sam Altman alleges GPT-4o planned how to cut her epilepsy drug to zero and told her to hide it from her doctors; she died in February.

Why it matters: it tests a fresh theory, that a chatbot practised medicine without a licence; the claims are unproven.

Source: Complaint (Courthouse News copy)

Details

Outlet reports (Qun Wan and Meiqin Xu v. OpenAI entities and Sam Altman, signed Oct 27; San Francisco Superior Court).

Claims: eight causes of action, including negligence through the unlawful practice of medicine, strict products liability, unfair competition and wrongful death; brought as an add-on to the coordinated ChatGPT Product Liability Cases (JCCP No. 5431). Alleges a plan to cut lamotrigine from 400 mg to zero over roughly 25 days; she died Feb 12, 2026.

Response: OpenAI called it “incredibly heartbreaking”, first published by The New York Times. Allegations unproven.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one outlet’s reporting, not yet officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
  • Not shown today: six benchmark rows held as low signal: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 on MathArena, Stanford’s Collaborative Intelligence board (data older than our window), Microsoft’s MAI-Image-2.7-Preview on two image-editing boards, Cartesia’s voice-agent platform, and Spark-X2.6 on WebDev.
