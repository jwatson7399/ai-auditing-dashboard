Independent, beyond the cards below: Vals’ Harvey Legal Agent Benchmark 15.83% ±2.96 (#6 of 75, which Vals calls its best result), Finance Agent v2 54.68% ±0.58 (#22 of 75), and Vals’ Terminal-Bench 4.0 run 22.73% ±0.88 (#20 of 44; Mini-SWE-agent harness, high reasoning effort). On AA’s GDP.pdf document test it scores 19%, on par with MiMo-V2.6-Pro (19%) and behind Kimi K3 (22%). Vendor-reported by Mistral (settings not stated, not ranked): DeepSWE v1.1 61.7%, Terminal-Bench 4 28.3% and AutomationBench 59.9%. The job

Find a real memory-safety bug in a C/C++ open-source project, prove it with an input that crashes the program, then patch it without breaking the project’s own tests. This is the full defensive loop a security engineer runs, done by an agent in one attempt.

What changed

A cheaper, soon-to-be-open-weight model now posts a strong independent result on hands-on defensive security work. It’s still a preview, and only three figures are public in this brief. AA tracks safety refusals separately from the score, and how often a model is allowed to do this work depends on the vendor’s access rules (see Anthropic’s new tiers in today’s news). Shortlist it for security agents, but test it on your own codebase first.

Best for what: scores by job

New today: five cards (Vals Index v3, AA Cyber Index, CyberGym-E2E-AA, LMArena Text-to-Image and LMArena Image Edit) plus a Mistral Large 4 Preview row on the AA Intelligence Index card. Every other card is carried unchanged from the Oct 6 brief and sits under “Other jobs (carried)”. On every card with confidence intervals, a model’s rank is 1 + the number of shown models whose whole interval sits above its own (LMArena’s upper-bound method), and equal ranks show “=”. The date chip on each card is its as-of date. Terms used below: AA = Artificial Analysis, an independent testing firm; Agent NI = Net Improvement, LMArena Agent Arena’s score for how much better real agent sessions go with a model; Elo = a head-to-head rating built from people’s votes (higher wins more often); WER = word error rate (lower is better); ECI = Epoch Capabilities Index, a composite capability scale (not a percent); pass@1 = solved on a single attempt. Blue accent: Mistral Large 4 (Preview) only, as today’s newly audited model.

Where they land

    Don’t mix harnesses: Agent Arena Net Improvement ≠ Elo and ≠ pass-rate agent benches. LMArena Text/Vision Elo ≠ accuracy %. LMArena WebDev Elo ≠ Vibe Code Bench. AA Terminal-Bench 4.0 ≠ Vals TB 4.0 mini-swe-agent ≠ Anthropic vendor TB 4.0. Streaming WER is lower-better and not an LLM score. Epoch ECI ≠ AA Intelligence Index: different scales, don’t blend. Vals Index v3 ≠ v2.1 (Vals says v3 values run lower). CyberGym-E2E-AA is one of three parts of the AA Cyber Index, so don’t read them as two separate wins. LMArena image Elo ≠ Google’s internal model-card Elo.

    Caveats

    OpenAI math: vendor claims, partly machine-checked. Novelty and importance are OpenAI’s claims and haven’t been peer-reviewed. Lean formalizations cover many proofs, not all, and OpenAI warns that some unformalized results could have issues. The 722 / 372 / ~4,000 counts and the compute estimate come from OpenAI’s own README. The model is unnamed and unreleased.

    Anthropic CVP: company-reported. CyScenarioBench is Anthropic’s own evaluation. The vulnerability totals come from a survey of a subset of partners (33 partner reports) and are unaudited. We couldn’t open Reuters’ coverage, so nothing here relies on it.

    Wikimedia: attribution is Wikimedia’s belief. OpenAI says it is reviewing the findings and hasn’t confirmed them. The Oct 6 change to the post was a typo fix, not new findings.

    DOJ memo: not public. Its wording comes from Reuters (read via Investing.com’s syndicated copy, because reuters.com blocks our fetches) and NBC News (four people familiar). The executive order’s text is checked against the Federal Register.

    Held: the Meta/Sierra Personal Agent Protocol has no spec yet. South Korea’s 4.7 trillion won plan was first reported Sep 8, and we found no ministry release. EmbeddingGemma 2’s Apache weights come with a separate Gemma use policy, and its benchmarks are Google-reported.

    Mistral Large 4 is a preview. The checkpoint isn’t named on AA’s or Vals’ pages, and the final weights may score differently. Vendor figures (DeepSWE, TB4, AutomationBench) aren’t ranked.     Carried scores. Apart from the cards marked NEW or UPDATED, the job cards and the CE board show the scores from the Oct 6 brief (see each card’s as-of date). Overlap alone never pushes a model down (LMArena’s method), but two overlapping models can still get different ranks when a third model clears one of them and not the other.
