OpenAI will vet companies that resell access to its AI

From Nov 20, routers, AI gateways and other services that resell direct access to OpenAI’s models need its prior approval.

Why it matters: those services now need the company’s sign-off; no application process is published yet.

Source: OpenAI — Usage Policies

Details

What it is: an update to the company’s Usage Policies (changelog dated Oct 21; effective Nov 20, 2026).

Who needs approval: anyone operating a “model-access intermediary platform or other service that enables third parties to directly access [its] models” must “obtain our prior review and approval.” Apps that merely build its models in are exempt.

Training ban: no using its models or outputs to prepare training data, feedback or rubrics for models “intended as alternatives to or substitutes for” its own.

Also: approval rules for national-security and law-enforcement uses; clarified child, teen and high-stakes protections.

Caveat: no application process or review criteria published yet.

Websites running PHP should update now

PHP fixed two critical flaws that let attackers run code on web servers without logging in.

Why it matters: if your site runs PHP, update today. OpenAI says an AI-assisted team found one flaw (its own account).

Source: PHP security advisory · OpenAI disclosure

Details

The flaws: CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, both CVSS v4.0 9.2; the project says all PHP installations serving web requests are at risk, mod_php most.

Fix: update to 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35 (released Oct 21).

AI angle (OpenAI’s account): Calif found one flaw using GPT-5.6 Sol through Patch the Planet; Wiz independently found both.

Caveat: the AI role rests on the company’s own account.

OpenAI asks for shutdown and incident-reporting rules

It proposes “reliable shutdown controls” and consistent incident reporting for frontier models and agents.

Why it matters: a lab asking for rules a day after disclosing its own agent incidents; proposals only.

Source: OpenAI (Oct 21)

Details

The Oct 21 post (“Democratic governance of frontier AI, part II”) suggests steps for governments and industry in three areas: securing frontier models and agents, modernizing cyber defense, and stronger government evaluations with “consistent incident reporting requirements.” OpenAI cites its own misalignment and security incidents.

Caveat: proposals only, not adopted policy.

DeepMind staff quit over military AI deals

The BBC reports several Google DeepMind staff have resigned or are leaving over Google’s military AI contracts.

Why it matters: visible staff pushback inside a frontier lab; no staff count given.

South Korea drafts rules on AI claims in ads

Its Fair Trade Commission seeks comment until Nov 11 on “AI washing” and AI-generated people, reviews and deepfakes in ads.

Why it matters: a regulator spelling out when “AI-powered” claims cross the line; not legally binding.

Source: Korea Fair Trade Commission

Details

Four violation types, 16 example cases and an 8-item self-check list; comments run Oct 22–Nov 11. The guidelines are not legally binding.

Caveat: per ZDNet Korea, a separate ban on ads with AI-generated virtual doctors or experts takes effect Nov 27.

How we rank + Sources

Independent boards first. On boards with error ranges, a model ranks below another only if its whole range sits lower; otherwise we call it tied.

How we rank
  • Top pick = rank 1 on that card. Runners show as “Next: …” — the next rank, or “(+n)” when more share it. On cards with intervals, rank = 1 + models whose whole interval sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”.
  • Tags (60-second): Confirmed = independent source · Vendor says = company’s own report · Outlet reports = one outlet’s report, not yet officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within intervals · Sources disagree = claims conflict.
  • Scoreboard symbols: ★ 1st or tied (“=”) · ● 2–5 · · lower · ○ not on this card · † preliminary or vendor-reported · ↑/↓ = rank change vs the week-ago brief.
  • Cost: CE (cost-effectiveness) = AA Intelligence Index ÷ AA $ per task (higher = more smarts per dollar). Best deal = each model’s best-CE setting.
  • Jargon: ±1 SE (binomial) = one-run error bar from a yes/no success rate · mini-swe-agent = the small agent harness Vals uses · AA = Artificial Analysis · Elo = vote-based preference rating · Agent NI = Agent Arena Net Improvement (not Elo) · WER = word error rate, lower is better · ECI = Epoch Capabilities Index.
  • Provider Adapter (ARC-AGI-3) = a harness that keeps the model’s reasoning state between turns; Standard harness keeps only notes the model chooses to save. Never mixed.
  • Each card keeps its own as-of date. Vendor-reported cards are labeled and never ranked across labs. Independent boards first (AA, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC). Don’t blend harnesses or scales.
