Lead · New York RAISE Act · Nov 10

New York opens frontier AI registration and a state AI research center

Large frontier AI developers must register on the state’s RAISE Portal by Jan 1, 2027; a Binghamton University research center lists independent testing of frontier systems among its aims.

Why it matters: from January, frontier developers must report critical safety incidents within 72 hours; the Attorney General can seek fines.

Source: Governor’s release · State regulator’s RAISE page · Penalty law §1427 (public.law copy)

Details

Confirmed (Governor’s release, Nov 10; Department of Financial Services page). The portal is run by the department’s Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity, and Trust.

Who is covered: a “large frontier developer” has more than $500M (USD) in yearly gross revenue with affiliates; a frontier model is trained with more than 1026 operations.

Duties from Jan 1, 2027: large frontier developers register by that date and publish safety frameworks; all frontier developers report critical safety incidents within 72 hours and publish model transparency reports. TechCrunch’s line that only large developers report incidents is narrower than the state’s page.

Penalties come from the law, not the release: the Attorney General may sue for up to $1M (USD) for a first violation and $3M for each later one (General Business Law §1427); §1428 adds $1,000 a day for operating without a current disclosure statement. These are ceilings; there is no private right to sue.

Research center: the New York Center for AI Responsibility and Research has interim co-directors Greg Morrisett (Cornell Tech) and Jeannette Wing (Columbia), more than 80 researchers and Empire AI computing; it was first announced in January with a $30M gift led by Tom Secunda plus $25M from SUNY (USD). No test results or start date for testing.

Co‑lead · Paris Call · signed Nov 9

16 governments and 10 AI developers sign the Paris Call for AI Safety and Security

Developers including OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft and Mistral AI commit to fail-safe shutdown mechanisms, including during training and internal use.

Why it matters: voluntary: no deadlines, enforcement or penalties; the US, Google and Meta are not listed.

Source: Élysée text

Details

Confirmed (Élysée, Nov 9; the signatory list is still being updated).

Governments (16): Austria, Bahrain, Canada, Croatia, Cyprus, Estonia, Finland, France, Ireland, Kenya, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal. Developers (10): Anthropic, Black Forest Labs, Cohere, Domyn, Hugging Face, Microsoft, Mistral AI, OpenAI, Sakana AI, Synthesia. Civil society (7): LawZero, SaferAI, AI Safety Connect, Ifri, Geopolitical Center for the Datasphere, GPAI Policy Lab, CeSIA.

All signers: a common evaluation and risk-management framework; publish non-sensitive evaluation findings in a standard format; independent third-party evaluation against standards set or recognized by national authorities.

Developers: disclose capabilities, limitations and known risks; support independent evaluation “where appropriate and proportionate to the risk”; fail-safe shutdown “within their control, including during training and internal use.”

Governments: confidential whistleblower channels; clear accountability for large-scale damage. Incident sharing is softer: signers “recognize the value” and are exploring a cooperation mechanism.

Also · EU AI Act · published Nov 11

EU sets detailed rules for AI Act regulatory sandboxes

Implementing Regulation 2026/2487, published Nov 11, sets how national AI test sandboxes run; it takes effect Dec 1.

Why it matters: small firms and start-ups get priority and free supervision; exit reports count EU-wide but do not prove compliance.

Source: EU Official Journal text (EUR-Lex)

Details

Confirmed (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2487 of Nov 10, Official Journal dated Nov 11). It enters into force on the twentieth day after publication, which counts to Dec 1, 2026.

Timing: a recital (background text, not a binding article) expects sandboxes in Member States by Aug 2, 2027.

Costs: small and medium-sized firms and start-ups are prioritised and pay nothing for guidance, advice and supervision (exceptional costs possible with advance notice); others may be charged full-cost fees from a published list.

Exit report: issued within 45 working days after participation ends; market surveillance authorities and notified bodies in every Member State “shall recognise” it. It is not a declaration of conformity and gives no presumption of conformity.

Also · Children’s AI charter · Nov 10

OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft and Black Forest Labs back a charter on safer AI for children

The Paris Peace Forum charter commits companies to robust testing before deployment where relevant and at regular intervals.

Why it matters: voluntary and separate from the Paris Call; it sets up no approval regime.

Source: Paris Peace Forum release · Charter (PDF, English)

Details

Confirmed (charter text and release, Nov 10). The charter’s endorsement line names all four companies; the release names only OpenAI, Anthropic and Common Sense Media, and says an 8 million euro research pledge “will be made” in total, without a split.

Commitments: companies “will carry out, before deployment where relevant and at regular intervals thereafter, robust evaluation and testing,” use age checks that do not rely only on self-declaration, and support collaboration with independent evaluators.

Research network: set up by the coalition of France, Spain and Kenya, not by the companies; industry money supports its Shared Measurement work, other parts are public or philanthropic.

Caveat: President Macron’s “tested, evaluated, and therefore authorized” goes beyond the text.

Also · Microsoft security research · Nov 10

Microsoft researchers hide a backdoor in AI agents by editing a few neurons

Microsoft Research reports that editing fewer than 10 dormant neurons can plant a hidden trigger in an AI agent model; the attacker needs write access to the model files.

Why it matters: a risk for anyone running third-party model files; Microsoft has seen no attacker use.

Source: Microsoft Research blog · Paper abstract · Microsoft Security blog

Details

Vendor says (Microsoft Research blog and abstract, Nov 10; authors Zeng, Severi, Bullwinkel, Horvitz, Papailiopoulos). Results are the authors’ own.

Main result: in Nemotron-Terminal-8B, 7 of more than 12,000 neurons, fitted on 23 example runs with and without the trigger, fire on at least 98.8% of held-out triggered runs and never on harmless ones. Qwen 3.6-27B and Gemma-4-31B-IT are also affected. The rates measure whether the model emits the attacker’s command in recorded sessions, not a completed attack.

Detection: detectors built by an AI agent flagged 91.4% of held-out edited models at a 9.7% false-alarm rate; reliable detection needs comparison with the original weights.

Other claims: Microsoft says Claude Code with Opus 4.5 found most of this on its own (the abstract says February, the blog says March). Microsoft’s Security blog says it has seen no threat actor use.
