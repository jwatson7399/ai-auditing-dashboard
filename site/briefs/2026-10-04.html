    Caveats

    Robinson essay is one former employee’s account. He says he “led the drafting of our current Preparedness Framework, and oversaw the writing of safety reports on 12 frontier launches,” and that he “resigned this week.” “Culture is broken” is the Atlantic headline’s wording, not a line in the essay body. He has hired a PR firm (Spitfire Strategies) and says “the decision to speak out is mine alone.” Separate from the Oct 1–3 WSJ report that OpenAI “parted ways” with three other safety researchers — don’t imply a link. OpenAI commented on the record (Drew Pusateri to TechCrunch); it did not decline.

    Muse relationship pages rest on instruction files, not observed profiles of real people. WIRED’s headline framing (“all your friends and family”) is the outlet’s. Meta (Daniel Roberts) is on record that Muse gathers context “based on public information and from what you’ve chosen to share.” Per-user VM and wipe/disconnect claims in the story are WIRED’s unattributed description — only confirmation-before-actions and the audit log are attributed to Meta in the piece.

    Kolibri licence is weights + config only. Apache-2.0 covers the published weights and configuration files; Aleph Alpha retains rights to code, architecture, parameter settings and training methods. All cited benches (GPQA Diamond, AIME 2025, SWE-Bench Verified, etc.) are vendor-run; no independent leaderboard entry yet. Blog says German share of pre-training is 21.3%; the model card says about 23.9%.

    Trillium funding figures are targets, not money raised: “an undisclosed sum from Schmidt Sciences, Halcyon Futures, and others”; founders aim to raise $40–100M and plan to spend $30M on training over 18 months (WIRED, Oct 2).

    Guided Vision is not for navigation. Google: available on Android 9+ where Gemini Live is supported; “not a medical device, mobility aid, or white cane replacement”; “not intended for navigation, safe-travel guidance, or obstacle detection.” Built with Aira.

    Bessent / Axios “propose” restates Sep 25. The White House fact sheet already says the U.S. and China “agreed to establish a bilateral communication channel for SI incidents.” “Propose” is Axios’s paraphrase; none of Bessent’s direct quotes name a notification system.

    Irving TIME essay is opinion, not news: former UK AISI chief scientist (now Resolution) puts ~50% chance “we all die” and calls to “stop frontier AI development immediately.” Personal estimate; TIME notes opinions need not reflect its editors.
