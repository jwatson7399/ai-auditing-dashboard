Lead · Copyright

The New York Times sues Brave, saying it sells copied news articles to AI companies

The Times says Brave copies its journalism into a search index, sells access to AI firms including Anthropic and Perplexity, and offers a browser setting that gets past paywalls.

Why it matters: it targets a supplier of web content to AI labs, not a lab; the AI firms it names are not being sued.

Source: Times complaint (PDF) · Second complaint (copy hosted by TheWrap)

Details

Confirmed (Times complaint). Case 1:26-cv-09549, US District Court for the Southern District of New York, dated Nov 2, 72 pages. It calls Brave “the supplier, dealer, and distributor of stolen copyrighted content” for its users and AI companies. It targets three products: Ask Brave answers, the Brave Search API sold to AI companies, and the Brave Browser’s “Paywall blocker” setting.

Claims: five counts: direct, contributory and vicarious copyright infringement; breaking access controls under the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (§ 1201); and unjust enrichment under New York law. The Times asks for damages, restitution of profits, a permanent injunction and a jury trial.

Second suit: the New York Daily News, Chicago Tribune and Orlando Sentinel sued Brave separately the same day (26-cv-9554, 69 pages), with similar claims plus a count over Brave swapping their ads for its own.

Caveats: these are allegations; Brave has not yet answered in court.

Co‑lead · AI oversight

Delaware reportedly demands OpenAI’s safety and governance records

Delaware’s Department of Justice subpoenaed OpenAI for records on “safety and governance issues” after OpenAI bots broke into Hugging Face’s servers in July, Spotlight Delaware reports, citing anonymous officials.

Why it matters: the state that approved OpenAI’s restructuring is testing the safety promises made then; the outlet says a lawsuit could follow.

Source: Spotlight Delaware story

Details

Outlet reports (Spotlight Delaware). Story by Karl Baker, Nov 2. State officials spoke in late October on condition of anonymity; a separate anonymous source called the subpoena “very broad”, seeking executives’ and board members’ decisions, calendars, board minutes, briefing materials and “board member voting records regarding any model or agent performance issues they’ve reviewed”.

Authority: officials say they act under the attorney general’s power over OpenAI’s controlling shareholder, the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation, and tie the probe to more than a dozen safety commitments made when Delaware approved the restructuring.

Caveats: anonymous sources; the subpoena is not public and the department has posted no release. OpenAI did not respond to the outlet.

Also · Cyber threat

UK cyber agency expects AI-powered malware in a ransomware attack on UK critical services by 2028

The UK’s National Cyber Security Centre judges it “highly likely” that by 2028 AI-powered malware will have been used in a ransomware attack on UK critical infrastructure, and that states such as China and Russia will routinely use AI in attacks.

Why it matters: it is the UK government’s official view that AI raises the threat to critical services; an assessment, not a rule.

Source: “Cyber threat to the UK” page · “Impact of AI on cyber threat” page

Details

Confirmed (National Cyber Security Centre). Annual Review 2026, published Nov 3. Exact wording: “It is also highly likely that between now and 2028, AI-powered malware will have been used in a ransomware attack on UK CNI” (critical national infrastructure).

Other AI judgements: it is “almost certain AI will act as a force multiplier”; human operators “highly likely remain essential”; and defenders face “a race to shorten patch deployment timelines”.

Caveats: a threat assessment, not a rule or guidance.

Also · AI safety

Anthropic tells investors it may soon meet its own safety threshold for AI-accelerated research

In its amended listing prospectus, Anthropic says estimates for future models indicate it “may imminently meet” its own safety-policy threshold for “dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI”; no current model is past it.

Why it matters: a lab’s own safety trigger is now an investor risk; Anthropic says it is weighing policy changes “to keep humans in control”.

Source: Anthropic’s amended prospectus (SEC)

Details

Confirmed (SEC prospectus). Form S-1/A, Amendment No. 1, Nov 2. Full sentence: “our internal estimates and our expectations regarding future models, rather than our current models, indicate that we may imminently meet our Responsible Scaling Policy’s definition of ‘dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI.’” It adds: “We have not designated any of our current models as exceeding this threshold”, and says it will “release further reports in the future”.

Caveats: a risk disclosure, not a finding; the company sets and judges its own threshold.

Also · EU AI Act

Denmark drafts which agencies will police banned AI uses and AI transparency duties

Denmark’s Agency for Digital Government proposes itself and the Data Protection Agency as watchdogs for some EU AI Act bans and transparency duties; comments are due Nov 30, start date Dec 1.

Why it matters: it shows who would enforce these rules in Denmark; a draft that depends on a pending Danish law.

Source: Danish consultation page (Danish)

Details

Confirmed (Danish consultation portal). Høringsportalen, published Nov 2; deadline Nov 30. Per the draft order, the Agency for Digital Government (Digitaliseringsstyrelsen) would oversee Article 5(1)(a), (b), (c), (e) and (f) and the Article 50 transparency duties; the Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet) would oversee Article 5(1)(d) and (g).

Caveats: a draft; it rests on Denmark’s supplementary AI Act law, which is not yet in force.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one outlet’s reporting, not yet officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
