News

Lead · AI security audits

India’s cyber emergency team sets out how AI systems should be audited

India’s cyber emergency team published AI security auditing guidelines with four risk tiers, from low to critical, and outside audits for all but the lowest.

Why it matters: it gives auditors a national checklist and a 6-hour clock for reporting AI security incidents.

Source: Guidelines (PDF, 107 pages) · Mint

Details

Confirmed (CERT-In, India’s Computer Emergency Response Team). AI Security Auditing Guidelines, Version 1.0, dated Nov 4.

Tiers: Tier 1 (low) allows self-assessment; Tiers 2–4 (moderate, high, critical) need independent audits by CERT-In empanelled auditing organisations.

Clocks: AI incidents meeting CERT-In reportable criteria “shall be reported within 6 hours”; logs are kept for at least 180 days.

Scope: the text covers organisations of all sizes operating AI in India or processing Indian citizens’ data, including foreign AI service providers.

Caveats: an audit framework, not a law. Outlet reports: per Mint (Nov 4), an unnamed official said the guidelines “are for auditors to use” and are not binding on AI firms.

Co‑lead · Facial recognition

The UK plans a law and an independent watchdog for police facial recognition

The Home Office says it intends to bring in a legal framework for police and public bodies using facial recognition and biometrics, with a watchdog that could seek court orders against forces breaking the rules.

Why it matters: police use would move from a patchwork of rules to one law with an enforcer; there is no bill or timetable yet.

Source: Home Office release · Government response (PDF)

Details

Confirmed (UK Home Office). Release dated Nov 4, 10:51 AM ET, with the government response to its consultation.

Planned: a specific law saying police use must be intelligence-led and proportionate; independent bias testing of police technologies; a legal code of practice for police, and a separate code for private-sector users such as shops.

The watchdog will “consolidate and expand the functions of existing oversight roles”; in extreme cases it could seek a court injunction stopping a force from using these technologies.

Caveats: a stated intention (the government “intends to introduce” the framework); no bill or timetable. Arrest and crime figures in the release are the government’s own.

Also · Copyright in court

A judge dismisses music publishers’ claims against Anthropic’s chief executive, for now

A federal judge in California dismissed music publishers’ copyright claims against Anthropic’s Dario Amodei personally; the publishers may amend them.

Why it matters: it tests whether AI company bosses can be personally liable; Anthropic’s own request is still undecided.

Source: Court docket (via CourtListener) · Courthouse News

Details

Confirmed (court docket). Concord Music Group v. Anthropic, Northern District of California, before Judge Eumi K. Lee.

Outlet reports (Courthouse News): at the Nov 4 hearing the judge granted Amodei’s motion with leave to amend, asked for more briefing on Anthropic’s motion, and found the publishers had not shown how Amodei committed direct infringement, and he may still face secondary infringement claims.

Also · Data centres

OpenAI pledges to publish water and power use at computing campuses it owns

OpenAI published a community compact for future campuses it owns and operates, promising site-level water and electricity figures and no non-disclosure agreements with officials, plus proposals for lawmakers.

Why it matters: voluntary pledges on the local effects of AI data centres.

Source: OpenAI’s blog post

Details

Vendor says (OpenAI). Post dated Nov 5.

Compact: a local compact for each future campus OpenAI owns and operates, annual public reporting, site-level water and electricity use, and no non-disclosure agreements with public officials beyond narrow security or trade-secret carve-outs.

Proposals for lawmakers: public reporting, “narrow limits” on when developers can require officials to sign non-disclosure agreements, public tax-incentive terms with clawbacks, and state utility commissions requiring large electricity customers to cover the grid costs they add.

Caveats: voluntary pledges and proposals.

Also · US AI oversight

A Pentagon official says the US will spell out which laws apply to AI labs within weeks

Reuters reports the Pentagon’s technology chief, Emil Michael, said a government AI task force will publish the laws that apply to US AI labs “in the next week or two”.

Why it matters: it could give labs a federal map of existing duties; for now, a promise with no official statement.

Source: Reuters story (via CNA)

Details

Outlet reports (Reuters). Story by Greg Bensinger, Nov 4, on remarks at a Wall Street Journal technology conference. Michael is vice chair of the Super Intelligence Task Force.

Quote: the aim is “a framework that makes the American people have confidence that these companies are going to do the right thing”. Per Reuters, he gave no further details.

Caveats: no official task-force statement; a promised publication, not a rule.
