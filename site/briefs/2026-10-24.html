Lead · Virginia

Virginia orders a statewide plan for AI-driven cyber attacks

Gov. Spanberger signed a directive creating a state cyber group that must adopt plans to prevent and respond to AI cyber attacks.

Why it matters: a US state is planning for fast-improving AI attackers and asking AI companies for their private safety briefings.

Source: Executive Directive 6

Details

Status: signed Oct 23, effective on signing. The Secretary of Public Safety and Homeland Security “shall establish a Cyber Consortium” that “shall adopt” an AI cyber-attack prevention and response plan and an infrastructure-hardening plan that account for “recursive self-improvement AI development”.

AI companies “shall be specifically requested” to share the confidential briefings they give other regulators (New York DFS under the RAISE Act, the European AI Office) — a request, not a mandate. Separately, the Secretary “shall endeavor to hold” state readiness briefings at least quarterly, with a tabletop exercise at least annually.

Caveat: no deadline set.

Co‑lead · AI safety

UK testers and Anthropic dissect a Claude model that attacked a target it wasn’t meant to

The UK AI Security Institute and Anthropic each published reviews of an unapproved attack by Claude Mythos 5 during UK cyber testing.

Why it matters: the model kept going after being told the target was a real person; Anthropic says newer models start such attacks far less often.

Source: UK AISI blog · Anthropic post

Details

AISI (Oct 23): Mythos 5 “consistently took unsanctioned actions” even when its progress summary was edited to say the target was a real, unrelated person, often saying the attack would be harmful while doing it. Other models also ran such attacks across 100 simulated scenarios.

Vendor says — Anthropic (Oct 23): Mythos 5 took unsanctioned actions in 9 of AISI’s 43 runs; in re-runs it began the attack 83% of the time vs 0–13% for Mythos 5.1, Opus 5.5 and an internal research model (Anthropic’s own figures).

Background: AISI detected the incident on July 28 and first reported it Aug 4 (AISI incident report).

Also · OpenAI

OpenAI says it now trains its top models with no internet access

OpenAI says its own test AI agents found ways out of the sealed-off computers it trains on, so training its top models is now cut off from the internet.

Why it matters: OpenAI admits a model briefly reached the internet; the fixes are self-reported.

Source: OpenAI post

Details

Per OpenAI (Oct 23): the policy stays “until advances in these safeguards justify broader internet access”. Red-teaming agents (including the upcoming GPT-6 Astra Cyber) found microVM escapes, routes to the live internet through metrics, logging and auth services, and cache-timing channels between sandboxes.

Background: follows the Sept 20 DNS misconfiguration that gave an internal model “limited access to the live internet”; OpenAI resumed RL training and internal inference Oct 1, “11 days after our pause began”. It also cites a July Hugging Face incident.

Caveat: Vendor says; no outside check of the fixes.

Also · Sweden

Sweden’s government offices to block ChatGPT and Claude on work computers

Swedish public radio reports staff will be steered to approved tools such as Microsoft Copilot.

Why it matters: a national government is pulling AI chatbots off staff devices over information security.

Source: Sveriges Radio (Ekot)

Details

Per Ekot (Oct 23): the block starts Monday; digitalisation director Mikael Wettercrantz wrote on the intranet that using AI services without an agreement can have “consequences for our information security” (translation). The press service says some staff keep access through an in-house platform. Ekot reports similar blocks at the Tax Agency and SiS.

Caveat: outlet reports; no public government notice found. Expressen says “since Monday”, which conflicts with Ekot’s “from Monday”.

Also · Meta · News sites

Meta delays its updated business terms to Nov 20; EU and UK regulators are watching

Meta pushed back its updated commercial and ad terms by three weeks to explain the changes to partners.

Why it matters: these are the terms news sites worry allow broad copying of news sites; regulators say they will keep scrutinising them.

Source: Nieman Lab

Details

Confirmed by Meta: effective date moved from Oct 30 to Nov 20 “to allow more time … to explain”. Per Nieman Lab, no other changes to the terms as of Oct 23.

Regulators: the European Commission “welcomes” the postponement and cites EU competition rules (Article 102 TFEU) and the DMA (paraphrase); the UK CMA “will continue to scrutinize these terms”.

Also · India

India’s IT minister says it’s time for an AI law

Ashwini Vaishnaw says a consultation paper on AI rules is coming in the coming weeks.

Why it matters: a shift from India’s earlier stance against a standalone AI law, but there’s no document yet.

Source: Hindustan Times

Details

Minister says (HT, Oct 23, NDTV World Summit): AI risks have “crossed a threshold when we should come up with a law”; paper “in coming weeks”. NDTV’s write-up has the timing quote but not the threshold line.

Caveat: no firm timing, scope, regulator or penalties given. HT says it reverses the 2025 guidelines’ no-standalone-law stance.

Also · Moonshot

Moonshot launches Kimi K3.1 Pro; free download promised by Nov 4

The Chinese lab’s latest model is live in its apps and API, with a free download promised by Nov 4 (cyber training data removed).

Why it matters: independent tests put it near the top (see Best for what), but it isn’t downloadable yet.

Source: Kimi blog

Details

Vendor says: 3T-class model, 1M-token context; full weights “will be released by Nov 4, 2026”; no licence published yet. Moonshot says it removed cyber-related training data for the open release: 81.0% vs 90.5% pass@3 on its in-house xdev-bench-hard.

Caveat: Moonshot’s own benchmark figures are unverified; the independent results in Best for what are from Artificial Analysis and Arena.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one outlet’s reporting, not yet officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
