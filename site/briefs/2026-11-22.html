Lead · AI hosting · Nov 22

OpenRouter drops three AI providers after researchers find prompts exposed

OpenRouter, which routes prompts to many AI hosts, says it dropped three providers, took 125 endpoints off its Zero Data Retention (no-storage) list and notified potentially affected customers.

Why it matters: security firm Dragos says its planted test prompts show data from at least three AI providers was exposed; no regulator has acted.

Source: OpenRouter blog · Dragos research · Together notice · Novita notice · Phala report

Details

Vendor says (OpenRouter blog, Nov 22; Dragos post by HD Moore, Nov 19). OpenRouter says the findings “did not show any breach or unauthorized access of OpenRouter” and does not name the three providers it dropped. It says “Not all affected providers have posted publicly.”

Dragos sent “approximately two million prompts” carrying unique web links and decoy credentials from July through November 2026. It says marketplace resellers “were able to view and retain customer prompts using standard logging capabilities”, and that a Chinese AI lab’s systems fetched the secret links from more than half of the prompts sent to its models between July and October, including roughly two-thirds of requests marked “no data collection” or Zero Data Retention. Dragos names no providers.

Three provider notices describe different incidents, so they need not be OpenRouter’s three: Together (Nov 21) says stored June–July prompt samples were downloaded after a credential was exposed in a public code repository; Novita (Nov 22) says some MiniMax requests from May 28 to Nov 11 were forwarded to MiniMax’s official service; Phala (Nov 10) says some DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 requests routed by OpenRouter from Oct 21 to Nov 8 (UTC) should be treated as exposed.

Co‑lead · US Senate · Nov 19 (catch-up)

Senate passes bill its sponsors say makes data centers pay more grid costs

The Senate passed H.R. 2347, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026, as amended, 62–33 at 10:02 PM ET on Nov 19.

Why it matters: it still needs House passage; a sponsor says data centers would pay about $55 billion toward power lines over a decade, a Grid Strategies projection.

Source: Senate floor log, Nov 19 · Sen. Heinrich statement · Grid Strategies summary

Details

Confirmed for the vote (Senate floor log). Sen. Heinrich, the energy committee’s ranking Democrat, says the bill will “make data centers pay their fair share of electricity and grid costs” and “must also pass the House of Representatives before heading to the President’s desk to be signed into law.”

His statement says data centers would “contribute approximately $55 billion toward transmission costs over the next decade — approximately $35 billion more than they would pay under current policy, according to Grid Strategies”; the Grid Strategies summary (paper dated Nov 2) uses the same figures and points to Section 2107. Its other figures are also outside projections: household savings of about $50–$78 a year by 2040 (Rhodium Group) and up to 105,600 jobs through 2040 (Employ America). The data-center terms are as the sponsor and Grid Strategies describe them.

Catch-up: the vote came after the Nov 19 brief.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
