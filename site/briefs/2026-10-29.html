Lead · US state law

New York bans risky AI companion features for children, starting Jan 1, 2027

Governor Hochul signed a law that bars companion chatbots from offering unsafe features to anyone under 18, or to any user not first checked as an adult.

Why it matters: it is signed law with real penalties, and the state attorney general can sue companies that break it.

Source: Governor’s release · Bill text (S.9051-B)

Details

Confirmed (Governor’s release, Oct 28; bill text S.9051-B). Unsafe features include bots pretending to be human, claiming relationships, promoting self-harm, eating disorders or drug use, encouraging secrecy, and sexual content.

Enforcement: effective Jan 1, 2027 (§3 of the bill); the attorney general may seek civil penalties of up to $25,000 (USD) per violation, damages, restitution, and destruction of unlawfully obtained data and of algorithms trained on it, and must run a public complaints website. Customer service, research and internal business uses are exempt.

Release says: companies must offer more than one age-check method, one not relying solely on government ID, and delete age data after use.

Co‑lead · EU AI Act

Poland’s AI watchdog can now inspect companies and fine them

Enforcement parts of Poland’s AI-systems act took effect Oct 28, giving its AI commission powers to inspect, open proceedings and issue fines.

Why it matters: Poland now has a national AI Act enforcer that can fine companies.

Source: AI commission notice (gov.pl, Polish)

Details

Confirmed (Dz.U. 2026 poz. 1003, published July 27, 2026; Art. 127(2): arts 8–18 and chapters 3–5, 8 and 9 apply three months after publication; KRiBSI notice, Oct 28).

Fines: Art. 104 sets no national figures; KRiBSI fines in the cases and amounts set by Chapter XII of the EU AI Act (Art. 99), converted to złoty at the NBP euro rate of Jan 28 each year. Art. 107 lets it cut a fine by 10–50% if warnings are acted on within 3 months.

First sitting: chaired by Pamela Krzypkowska; online forms for opinion requests and complaints go live Oct 29; first guidance will cover AI Act Article 50 (telling people they are dealing with AI, labelling).

Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon goes to cyber defenders first

Google says Carbon starts with a small group of cyber defenders, then trusted partners, while it takes part in the US government’s voluntary pre-release testing.

Why it matters: most people can’t use it yet.

Source: Google blog

Details

Vendor says (Google blog, Koray Kavukcuoglu, Oct 28). Access goes to a subset of cyber defenders, then trusted partners through the Fairwind Program; wider access “as soon as possible”.

Price: $2/$10 (USD) per million input/output tokens at launch, then $4/$20.

Google’s figures (“evaluated without guardrails”): SEC-bench Pro 88.2%, ExploitGym 54.8%, CWE-bench 71.0%. No model card yet. Independent boards are in Best for what.

Also · Competition

US Justice Department is asking questions about Nvidia’s Hugging Face deal

Reuters, citing two people familiar, says the DOJ has asked whether Nvidia’s $12.93 billion purchase of Hugging Face would hurt competition in AI.

Why it matters: Hugging Face is where most open AI models are shared, so control of it matters; these are questions, not a case.

Source: Reuters story

Details

Outlet reports (Reuters exclusive, Oct 28, two people familiar). Officials asked whether the deal would let Nvidia “capture a fundamental distribution point for AI”, within a broader probe of Nvidia’s deals and sales practices.

Responses: DOJ and Nvidia declined to comment; Hugging Face did not respond. Reuters notes probes are not evidence of wrongdoing. Deal expected to close in the first half of 2027.

Also · Oversight

Sen. Warren presses Anthropic on risks it disclosed ahead of its IPO

Senator Elizabeth Warren sent Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei an 11-page letter this week, asking 10 multipart questions with answers due Nov 3.

Why it matters: it puts AI-safety risks in front of investors before Anthropic’s stock is sold; the letter has no legal force.

Source: Spectrum News

Details

Outlet reports (Spectrum News and CFO Dive, Oct 28).

Asks about: risk factors in Anthropic’s IPO prospectus (existential risk, self-preserving behaviour, weapons uplift, insurance limits), undisclosed incidents, and index funds buying the stock quickly.

Caveat: outlets give different letter dates (Oct 27 or 28).

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one outlet’s reporting, not yet officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
