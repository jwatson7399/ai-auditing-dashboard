Lead · Research · Nov 12

Report says six Chinese free-to-download models were started on export-controlled NVIDIA chips

Will DePue of Unemployment Labs says starting-weight patterns in DeepSeek, Kimi, MiniMax and Ant Group models match NVIDIA Blackwell systems.

Why it matters: by its own account it shows which chips started the models, not where they were or that any export rule was broken.

Source: Unemployment Labs report · DePue’s X thread (Thread Reader copy)

Details

Confirmed as to the report (dated Nov 12, revised the same day); the findings are the author’s.

Models: DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiniMax-M3 match B200/B300 systems; DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1-Pro and Ling-3.1-flash match GB200/GB300. Physical-chip confirmation is stated for V4-Flash and Kimi K3 only.

Strength: the strongest matches are 49 to 1,385 standard deviations, against about 8 expected by chance. The method agreed with all 24 models whose makers disclosed their hardware; the report calls that a consistency check, not a measured accuracy rate.

Limits: the fingerprint “supports, without proving,” that Blackwell also did the training; Blackwell systems “can be rented from data centers outside China.” Chip counts are the author’s revised estimates: about 4,500 for Kimi K3 and 3,000 for DeepSeek-V4-Pro, roughly $200–300 million for the largest cluster.

A claim that OpenAI and Anthropic researchers checked the work is in the X thread only; neither lab has confirmed it.

Co‑lead · US Congress · Nov 12

Two senior Democrats open a probe of the Nvidia China chip deal

Sen. Ron Wyden and Rep. Richard Neal, top Democrats on the tax committees, are investigating the tariff arrangement for Nvidia AI-chip sales to Chinese companies and asked Commerce to preserve records.

Why it matters: it is a request to keep records, not a subpoena, adding to the House chairs’ Dec 1–3 NVIDIA sessions (see Nov 13).

Source: Senate Finance release · Letter to Lutnick (PDF)

Details

Confirmed as to the letter and release (both Nov 12). Wyden and Neal are ranking members, not chairs.

Records asked for: the Section 232 chip investigation and resulting tariffs; Nvidia export-license decisions; and communications with or about Nvidia or Jensen Huang.

The deal, per the letter: Taiwan-made H200 chips pass through the United States for a security review, with a 25% tariff collected on entry, before export licenses; permission was granted in December 2025.

Outlet reports: the letter cites press reports for claims about briefings on Huang, a paused smuggling inquiry and at least $26 million Huang and Nvidia directed to the President’s priorities; those claims are not independently established.

Also · White House · met Nov 12

White House AI task force weighs legal cover for reporting AI cyberattacks

Reuters says Trump’s AI task force discussed ways to get critical-infrastructure operators to report AI-powered cyberattacks without fear of legal consequences.

Why it matters: the group recommended liability limits for at least 12 months; a 2015 cyber information-sharing law expires next month.

Source: Reuters (via CNA) · Cybersecurity Dive

Details

Outlet reports (Reuters, Nov 13, from an administration spokesperson). The group met on Thursday, Nov 12.

Vice chair Emil Michael cited progress on national standards for AI developers to report safety incidents.

Readout, as quoted by Cybersecurity Dive: agencies should limit regulatory liability for good-faith voluntary reporting of cyber weaknesses and incidents for at least 12 months; the group backs long-term renewal of the 2015 Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act. We did not open an official White House copy.

Also · California · Nov 13

California insurance regulator starts an AI survey and a Dec 15 workshop

Insurance Commissioner Lara launched an AI survey of insurers and a Dec 15 online workshop to inform possible consumer safeguards.

Why it matters: it is not a proposed rule and changes no current policies, coverage or rates.

Source: California Department of Insurance release

Details

Confirmed (release, Nov 13). The survey is called Responsible Use of AI.

Dates: workshop Dec 15 from 10 a.m. PST; written comments due Dec 12; survey answers due Dec 30.

Who gets the survey: companies writing at least $250 million a year in California premiums in 2025, plus selected InsurTechs and companies selling through managing general agents.

Any later formal proposal would have its own comment period.

Also · Anthropic · Nov 13

Anthropic paused many internet-connected safety tests after its own incidents

In guidance on building safety evaluations, Anthropic says it hardened its test setups and “temporarily stopped running many evaluations that give models internet access.”

Why it matters: it does not say what the incidents were; it advises running safety tests offline and watching them live.

Source: Anthropic research post

Details

Vendor says (post, Nov 13). Web-search tests that still need internet access now run with real-time monitoring.

For multi-turn safety tests, one bad turn fails the whole conversation.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one or more outlets’ reporting, not officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges or too close to separate.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
