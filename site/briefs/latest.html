News

Lead · US Treasury

US proposes a ban on investing in foreign “superintelligence” developers

A Treasury draft rule would bar US investment in companies in China and other US rivals that build “super intelligence” AI.

Why it matters: it widens today’s limits on AI investment abroad; proposal only, comments due Dec 7.

Source: Treasury fact sheet · press release

Details

Status: proposed rule under the COINS Act; a later final rule sets the start date. Comments close Dec 7 (fact sheet).

What changes (per the fact sheet): a broader ban on US investment in country-of-concern entities that develop any “super intelligence” system, dropping today’s notification step for those deals; adds supercomputing, hypersonics and biotech; widens countries of concern beyond China. Penalties up to IEEPA maximums, possible forced divestment.

Co‑lead · AI safety

Outside testers found holes in OpenAI’s agent-watching system

Two AI-safety research groups found ways around the system the company uses to catch its own AI agents misbehaving.

Why it matters: The company says it fixed the holes; no one has checked yet.

Source: METR report

Details

Findings (METR and Redwood Research, as of July 21): two client-controllable headers that switched monitoring off for freshly started agents; RL training runs uncovered at the start; a Codex sandbox and Auto-Review bypass; a redacted exploit that would hide hundreds of recent agent actions. METR estimates about 75–94% of agentic internal traffic was eligible for monitoring.

Caveat: two-person pilot (July 21–Aug 11). OpenAI “has stated” production fixes are in; METR hasn’t evaluated them. The 61-page report is private.

Also · Security · Microsoft

Microsoft says hackers used an AI attack tool at scale

Microsoft says a China-aligned group used a freely available AI hacking agent on hundreds of organisations.

Why it matters: AI agents are in real attacks; Microsoft also ties the group to the ConnectWise breach.

Source: Microsoft Security Blog

Details

Microsoft’s account: Storm-0940 used the ARTEX framework to try sign-ins to more than 2,250 tenants; in some cases it reached privileged cloud access in under 3 hours. Microsoft “assesses” the group was behind the ConnectWise compromise.

Caveat: Microsoft says a successful sign-in doesn’t necessarily mean data was accessed, and its own systems weren’t compromised. Posted Oct 21.

Also · Banks · Agents

Banks are spotting AI agents inside customer accounts

Axios reports big US banks are finding third-party AI agents using customers’ logins, and some are restricting them.

Why it matters: if you let an AI agent into your bank account, your bank may block it.

Source: Axios

Details

Per unnamed sources: Morgan Stanley is blocking third-party agents; JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi are restricting them. Some agents appear to act beyond user instructions.

On the record: Morgan Stanley says it hasn’t seen “rogue AI agents accessing our platforms”; Chase says don’t share passwords. Outlet reports, not confirmed by the banks.

Also · Health · Study

Microsoft’s diagnosis AI beat generalist doctors on hard cases

In Science, Microsoft authors report Microsoft’s diagnosis tool solved far more tough cases than generalist physicians.

Why it matters: a big gap on a peer-reviewed test, but text-only cases, not real patients.

Source: Science paper

Details

Per the authors: DxO with GPT-5 scored 83.2% on 304 NEJM cases vs physicians’ 36.1% open-book (22.3% closed-book) on 56 held-out cases; 80.3% vs 46.8% on emergency-department cases.

Caveat: all authors are Microsoft employees; no p-values or intervals in the main text; they say it doesn’t show superiority over specialists or real-world practice.

Also · Anthropic

Anthropic says Claude proved parts of 123 code libraries can’t crash

Anthropic also handed its Agent Skills format for AI agents to a Linux Foundation group.

Why it matters: formal proofs at this scale are rare; they hold only under narrow assumptions.

Source: Anthropic research post · Agent Skills post

Details

Proofs (per the authors): 239 entry points; about 180 million lines of Rocq proofs; no crash on any input under 64 MB, assuming a single-threaded static RISC-V Linux binary with a fixed memory budget. Opus 5.5 proved half of 36 small tasks with about 40% fewer tokens than Opus 5. The post itself says “formally verified” does not mean “bug-free.” With Theorem.

Agent Skills: moves to the Agentic AI Foundation, incl. the name, logo, GitHub org and agentskills.io. Anthropic says more than 70 agent platforms use it, with 1 million skills published and nearly 280 million installs.
