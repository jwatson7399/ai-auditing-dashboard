Lead · Finance rules

Hong Kong’s central bank starts a project on AI agents in banking and asks banks for views

Hong Kong’s central bank launched a project on AI agents that act for customers in finance, set up an expert group, and published a discussion paper asking banks how such agents should be supervised.

Why it matters: a major financial regulator is weighing rules written for AI agents, such as how banks check which agents to trust; for now it is a consultation, not a rule.

Source: Hong Kong Monetary Authority release · FinTech Week release

Details

Confirmed (Hong Kong Monetary Authority, HKMA, releases dated Nov 2, Hong Kong time). Project SAGE (“Securing Agentic Finance with Governance and Enablement”) has four workstreams: use cases (consumer, business and machine-to-machine), agent identity including “Know Your Agent” (KYA) practices and authentication, interoperability standards, and legal and regulatory questions including liability and consumer protection. The cross-sector Agentic Finance Expert Group draws on financial institutions, tech providers, professional firms and academia.

Discussion paper: per the FinTech Week release, it seeks “industry feedback on the supervisory considerations” for banks’ use of agentic AI. HKMA Chief Executive Eddie Yue told reporters it asks whether banks need a central registry of trusted agents and a KYA standard.

Caveats: non-binding; HKMA’s Oct 15 concept note says the work “does not constitute specific supervisory requirements at this stage”. The releases give no comment deadline.

Co‑lead · Government AI

Abu Dhabi and OpenAI turn government rules into a form AI can check permits against

Abu Dhabi’s government and OpenAI launched a “Machine-Readable Government” initiative so AI can help officials with routine compliance checks for permits and certificates; the first pilot already runs at Abu Dhabi’s food-safety agency, on food imports.

Why it matters: a government is putting AI into regulatory decisions, with each check traced to the rule behind it and officials keeping the final say.

Source: Abu Dhabi Media Office release

Details

Confirmed (Abu Dhabi Media Office and the WAM news agency, Nov 2). The initiative translates “a defined set of government policies and regulations” into machine-readable form; each assessment “is traceable to the governing rule”, and officials “retain oversight and accountability for final decisions”. It is led by Abu Dhabi’s Department of Government Enablement (DGE); the pilot is with the Abu Dhabi Agriculture and Food Safety Authority (ADAFSA) and runs on OpenAI’s in-country UAE computing.

Also signed: agreements with ADNOC and Inception42 on in-country AI computing, with Mubadala and Inception42 on investment analysis, and a five-year Inception42 adoption pact; no financial terms given.

Outlet reports (TechAfrica, citing a fuller DGE text): routine cases can be processed automatically, but not decisions affecting individuals’ rights or essential services, and data is not used for training. OpenAI has posted no statement; CNBC links the deals to OpenAI’s funding round, which the official text does not mention.

Also · US policy

Axios: OpenAI plans a policy push after the midterms, including “speed limits” for the most powerful AI

OpenAI will propose mandatory safeguards it calls “speed limits” for the most powerful AI systems and state bills giving communities a say in data-centre projects, starting when Congress’s post-election session opens on Nov 9, Axios reports.

Why it matters: OpenAI’s policy chief told Axios it will ask for binding limits on top AI systems; these are plans only, with no proposal text yet.

Source: Axios story

Details

Outlet reports (Axios “Behind the Curtain”, Jim VandeHei and Mike Allen, Nov 1), with on-record quotes from OpenAI’s chief global affairs officer Chris Lehane: “You’re going to see us talk more and more about the concept of speed limits.” Axios says OpenAI will also push to rename data centres “AI compute campuses”, alongside economic-opportunity and international-standards pushes, starting in the lame-duck session that opens Nov 9.

Caveats: no proposal text and no OpenAI post. Axios discloses a content licensing and technology agreement with OpenAI.

Also · Online harms

WIRED: about 400 ordinary-looking AI apps and websites hide tools for sexual deepfakes

A WIRED investigation with two research groups found a network of over 400 linked apps and websites posing as everyday AI tools, such as pet portraits and tutoring, that load a hidden interface for making sexually abusive deepfakes.

Why it matters: it shows gaps in app-store review and ad checks; Apple removed 19 apps and Meta acted against 11,600 ad accounts after WIRED asked.

Source: WIRED story

Details

Outlet reports (WIRED, Matt Burgess, Nov 2, 5:00 AM ET), with researchers Paul Bouchaud (AI Forensics) and Maël Le Touz (Infoblox), as quoted by WIRED. 27 of the apps were live on Google Play in September with over 350,000 downloads combined; Le Touz identified almost 500 linked domains; the network was promoted through tens of thousands of Meta ads.

On record: Apple removed 19 apps and says developers swapped content after review; Meta says it acted against 11,600 ad accounts and blocked 209 apps from advertising. Google did not comment.

Caveats: the link to Hong Kong-registered KeLing Technology Limited rests on WIRED’s domain review; KeLing did not respond.
