Lead · Lawsuit · Nov 12

Four nonprofits sue to freeze the most capable AI at five companies

Public Citizen and three other groups asked a San Francisco court to bar Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX Super Intelligence from building or releasing AI more capable than their best systems today, and from letting AI run that research itself.

Why it matters: it asks for court orders, not money; the claims are unproven and no hearing date is public.

Source: Complaint (PDF, posted by plaintiffs’ lawyers) · Public Citizen release

Details

Confirmed as to the complaint (dated Nov 12, 63 pages, San Francisco Superior Court). The posted copy leaves the case number blank; no court docket or hearing date was found.

Parties: Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and The Alliance for Secure AI, against Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI Group and the OpenAI Foundation, and two SpaceX Super Intelligence companies.

Legal basis: one claim under California’s Unfair Competition Law, arguing the work is a public nuisance and an unfair practice.

Asks: immediate court orders freezing capability at the level of each company’s best system on the complaint date and barring AI-run research toward more capable systems; legal fees. No damages.

Co‑lead · NVIDIA · Nov 11–12

NVIDIA’s Jensen Huang resigns; House chairs want him interviewed

NVIDIA’s board accepted Huang’s resignation as chief executive and director, named finance chief Colette Kress interim chief executive and Harvey Jones board chair; three House committee chairs asked Huang for a closed-door interview on Dec 1.

Why it matters: the chairs gave him until 8 p.m. ET tonight to confirm in writing, “or a subpoena will be issued.”

Source: NVIDIA release · SEC Form 8-K · House committee release · Letter to Huang (PDF)

Details

Confirmed (NVIDIA release and Form 8-K, Nov 12; the board acted Nov 11). Kress keeps the finance role while the board searches for a permanent chief executive. Neither document gives a reason.

Congress: the letter from Chairs Mast, Moolenaar and Crawford (Nov 12) sets the Dec 1 interview. A second letter asks NVIDIA’s general counsel, Tim Teter, for a Dec 2 interview on chip smuggling and export controls, with the same deadline. Chair Mast separately asked Commerce Under Secretary Jeff Kessler to appear at a Foreign Affairs hearing on Dec 3.

Outlet reports: TechCrunch ties the resignation to Huang’s admitted affair with an alleged Chinese spy; that is not in NVIDIA’s documents. Its timeline says Huang later agreed to testify before Thanksgiving; no official confirmation found.

Also · US Congress · introduced Nov 10

House bill would require national-security testing before powerful AI launches

H.R. 10981, the American AI Security Act from Reps. Gottheimer and Lawler, would make developers of covered models give the National Security Agency full access for a 30-day security review, extendable by 30 days, before release.

Why it matters: fines would reach $500,000 a day; the bill is only introduced, with no committee action.

Source: Bill text (GovInfo) · Bill record

Details

Confirmed (bill text as introduced, Nov 10; sent to the Energy and Commerce and Intelligence committees).

Who is covered: Commerce, with Defense through the agency’s director, would set criteria within 6 months: a minimum model size plus cyber or chemical, biological or radiological capabilities.

Release rule: no release unless the agency has evaluated the model and the developer has applied its recommendations, or the agency ran out of time.

Other terms: evaluations exempt from public-records requests; developers could appeal to a five-judge AI Security Review Court.

Also · Google · Nov 12

Google says Gemini 4 Hydrogen hits early-warning levels for bio and cyber risk

Its model card says targeted tests found Hydrogen below the critical levels in Google’s safety framework for biological and cybersecurity harm, but at the alert thresholds for both.

Why it matters: this is Google’s own testing; its fuller safety report is “coming soon.”

Source: Model card · Model card (PDF)

Details

Vendor says (model card, Nov 12). Hydrogen is adapted from Gemini 4 Carbon. Google ran the targeted tests because it “could not rule out” that Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon had reached those critical levels.

Refusals: Hydrogen refuses harmless requests more often than Gemini 3.8 Flash (5.57 points worse); the card says these figures are not directly comparable with earlier Gemini model cards.
