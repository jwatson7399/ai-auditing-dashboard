Lead · Northern Ireland AI rules · Nov 4

Limited EU AI Act duties are due to start in Northern Ireland on Tuesday

UK guidance published Nov 4 says limited EU AI Act rules are due to apply in Northern Ireland from Nov 10, after a planned Joint Committee decision, for AI closely integrated into physical products sold there — not standalone apps or direct duties on general-purpose model providers.

Why it matters: sellers of products with built-in AI face phased duties; intimate-image and child-abuse-imagery bans follow Dec 2.

Source: UK guidance (PDF) · GOV.UK publication

Details

Confirmed (UK government). Guidance dated Nov 4; Written Statement HCWS455 the same day. The Nov 10 start follows the planned Joint Committee decision; these duties are not yet active.

Scope: under the Windsor Framework, the Act applies only where AI is closely integrated with a tangible product placed on the Northern Ireland market. Standalone software, web apps and general-purpose model providers’ direct obligations are generally out of scope.

Dates: Article 5 market-placement prohibitions, Article 4 AI literacy and Article 50(1) interaction disclosure start Nov 10. Prohibitions on generating non-consensual intimate imagery and child-abuse imagery, and Article 50(2) output marking, start Dec 2. Annex III high-risk requirements follow Dec 2, 2027; Annex I high-risk Aug 2, 2028.

Caveats: the Joint Committee decision is planned, not yet adopted.

Co‑lead · Korea public-sector AI · Nov 5

South Korea requires public institutions to list the AI services they use

A Ministry of the Interior and Safety notice, effective Nov 5, requires public institutions to keep inventories of their AI services and submit them each year; the ministry publishes the lists, minus protected and security-sensitive details.

Why it matters: AI that public bodies buy or subscribe to is covered, so suppliers’ services can appear in published government lists.

Source: Ministry notice text (PDF, Korean) · Ministry notice board

Details

Confirmed (Korean government). Notice 2026-68, enacted and effective Nov 5, posted Nov 6. It implements Article 30 of the existing Act on Activation of Artificial Intelligence and Data-Based Administration; it is not a National Assembly act and does not cover private developers generally.

Coverage: each institution names an inventory officer. Lists cover public-facing AI, AI used in administrative decisions or judgments on rights, benefits or sanctions, internal automation, and acquired or subscribed AI. Each entry records purpose, operating method, data categories, effects on rights or decisions, and start date.

Cycle: Sept 30 snapshot, submission by end-October, gazette publication by Dec 31; specified material changes go in without delay. Protected personal information, trade secrets and security-sensitive material are excluded from publication.

Caveats: Korean text only; no official English version located. The notice sets no special first-year timetable.

Also · Dominican government AI · Nov 3

Dominican Republic sets binding ethics rules for government AI

Decree 787-26 requires public bodies and public companies to assess high-impact AI before use, tell people when AI supported a decision, offer an explanation and human review, and register their systems.

Why it matters: suppliers to Dominican public bodies face compliance clauses in contracts and must provide traceability.

Source: Official decree and annex (PDF, in Spanish) · Presidency announcement (in Spanish)

Details

Confirmed (Presidency of the Dominican Republic). Announced Nov 3; signed decree and annex posted on the Presidency site.

Scope: central and decentralized public administration and public companies. High-impact systems — those affecting access to rights, social benefits or sanctions — need an Ethics and Rights Impact Assessment before going live.

Timing: Article 14 gives 180 calendar days to adapt, counted from official publication, not signing. Existing high-impact systems are assessed by a deadline set by DIGEIG; human oversight and rights and data protection continue during adaptation.

Caveats: the official publication date is not yet confirmed, so no end date is given here.

Also · AI music lawsuit · Nov 6

Elvis rightsholders sue Suno over voice and copyright claims

Elvis Presley Enterprises and other rightsholders sued AI music company Suno in federal court in California, alleging it exploited Elvis’s voice and likeness and copied protected works.

Why it matters: Tennessee’s ELVIS Act voice-likeness law is being tested against an AI music generator; these are allegations, not findings.

Source: Court complaint (PDF, WREG-hosted copy)

Details

Confirmed (court complaint). Elvis Presley Enterprises, LLC et al. v. Suno, Inc., N.D. Cal. No. 3:26-cv-13398, dated Nov 6. Plaintiffs include Elvis Presley Enterprises, ABG EPE IP and Raleigh Music Publishing.

Claims: Tennessee ELVIS Act, Tennessee common-law right of publicity, direct copyright infringement, circumventing technical protections (§1201) and removing copyright information (§1202). Plaintiffs’ interests include song copyrights, not only Elvis recordings; listed works include non-Elvis songs. They seek damages and injunctions; nothing has been awarded.

Outlet reports (Variety, Nov 7): Eminem’s publisher Eight Mile Style brought a separate suit against Suno the same day; Eminem is not a plaintiff. Suno told Variety it bars unauthorized use of artists’ work and prompts naming specific artists or songs. Variety report

Caveats: allegations only; the complaint copy is hosted by WREG.
