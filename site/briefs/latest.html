Can I cut AI costs by using a lower reasoning setting? Vals, Nov 3, the top model from each of 7 labs: at the lowest setting, 4 of 7 kept at least 80% of their Vals Index score for half the cost or less (Claude Opus 5.5: 80% at 16% of the cost; GPT-6 Astra: 88% at 18%; Gemini 4 Argon: 99% at 84%). Lead · Government AI rules

The Dominican Republic makes an AI ethics code binding on its public bodies

By Decree 787-26, the government approved a Code of Ethics for AI in Public Services: high-impact systems need an ethics and rights assessment and approval before going live, and several uses are banned outright.

Why it matters: it is a binding national rulebook for government AI, not voluntary guidance.

Source: Presidency release (Spanish) · Decree 787-26 entry (Spanish)

Details

Confirmed (Presidency of the Dominican Republic). Release dated Nov 3, 9:25 PM local time (8:25 PM ET). It covers all central and decentralised public administration bodies.

High-impact systems (those affecting access to rights, social benefits or sanctioning powers) need an Ethics and Rights Impact Assessment and approval from the oversight body before going into production.

Citizens get a right to know when AI backed a decision, to an explanation, and to human review.

Banned: generalised social scoring, mass biometric identification in public spaces without a court order, predictive criminal profiling based on territorial or socioeconomic bias, automated criminal sentencing, and autonomous weapons. Systems aimed at children carry a reinforced presumption of risk.

Also: a consultative National AI Ethics Council. The code was drafted by the government IT office and its ethics directorate, with technical support from the Open Government Partnership.

Caveats: the release calls the code a regional benchmark; that is the government’s own description.

Co‑lead · AI law

China’s planning agency says it will speed up a broad national AI law

An official at China’s National Development and Reform Commission said China will “accelerate” foundational, comprehensive AI legislation during the current five-year plan and back sector “regulatory sandboxes” for testing AI.

Why it matters: it signals one comprehensive AI law plus test zones; a stated plan only, with no bill text or timeline.

Source: Beijing Daily (Chinese) · Xinhua (Chinese) · Commission press-conference notice (Chinese)

Details

Outlet reports. The commission’s notice for its Nov 3 press conference lists Wang Ruomeng, deputy director of its Department of Innovation and High-Tech Development, among the speakers; his remarks are as reported by Beijing Daily and Xinhua.

What he said: China will “accelerate” (加快推进) foundational, comprehensive AI legislation; add rules for data, compute, algorithms and key uses; set safety standards and ethics norms across the full AI lifecycle, reviewed periodically; give transition periods for rules; support sector sandboxes with application, testing and exit rules; and push developers to disclose risks promptly and report security incidents and vulnerabilities.

Also, per Xinhua: 38 countries have joined the World AI Cooperation Organization as founding members.

Caveats: a plan, not a draft law; no sectors or dates were given.

Also · Chip exports

Oracle reportedly drops a chip-rental deal with Tencent after US officials objected

The Financial Times reports, citing two people familiar with the matter, that Oracle cancelled a five-year lease worth an estimated $7 billion that would have given Tencent up to about 100,000 advanced AI chips in South-east Asia.

Why it matters: informal US pressure may be closing the route of renting chips abroad around export controls.

Source: Financial Times story (paywalled) · Reuters summary (via The Business Times)

Details

Outlet reports. Financial Times. Story dated Nov 4. Per one of the people, top officials from Guangdong, where Tencent is based, visited the company and asked it to support Chinese-made chips.

Context: the FT separately reported on Nov 3 (summary via The Next Web) that Nvidia told cloud partners to keep Chinese business under 49% of their capacity.

Caveats: anonymous sources and no formal government action; the $7 billion (US dollars) is the FT’s estimate; the chips sit in South-east Asian data centres. Reuters could not verify the report, and Oracle, Tencent and the White House did not respond.

Also · Children and AI

Google will switch off Gemini and Search for school student accounts until schools opt in

Google says its Gemini assistant and Google Search will be “entirely off” for school student accounts, kindergarten to 12th grade, until administrators “explicitly turn them on”.

Why it matters: AI for children becomes off by default on a major school platform; Google has not given a date.

Source: Google’s blog post

Details

Vendor says. Post by Chris Phillips, Google’s vice-president for education, Nov 3. Google says it will share specifics with schools “in the coming months ahead of the changes rolling out”.

Same post: Guided Learning is available worldwide in Google Classroom from Nov 3, and a Guided Learning pilot in Search starts in some US schools later this month.

Caveat: reports that schools blocking AI lose Search, and of a 2027 date, come from the New York Times and AP, not Google.

Also · Lab staff speech

Google DeepMind staff push to drop lifelong bans on criticising the company

The Washington Post reports that some London Google DeepMind staff have clauses with no end date barring them from criticising the company; Google says recent hires’ terms dropped the part that applies after leaving.

Why it matters: it limits what lab staff can say publicly about AI risks; current staff want the same change in their contracts.

Source: Washington Post story (paywalled)

Details

Outlet reports. Washington Post. Story by Gerrit De Vynck, Nov 3, 11:42 AM ET, based on a contract the Post reviewed and two current employees who spoke anonymously; they say the clauses stop them warning the public about AI risks.

On record: a Google spokesperson said the update removed “the post-termination aspect of non-disparagement clauses”. Per the Post, a Google executive told staff internally on Friday, Oct 30, that the clause would not be enforced against former employees.

Background: staff checked their contracts after former employee Sofia Liguori told the BBC she had ignored hers.

Caveats: anonymous employee sources; no sign Google will change existing contracts.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one outlet’s reporting, not yet officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
