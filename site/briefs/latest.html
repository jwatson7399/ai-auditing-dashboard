News

Lead · US Congress · Nov 19

House committee releases licence records for AI chips approved for China

The House Foreign Affairs Committee released US Commerce Department records showing it had approved 879,868 Nvidia and AMD chips for Chinese companies as of May 11, out of 3,444,160 requested.

Why it matters: every approved entry is marked “Approved, not shipped” as of May 11, not today; Nvidia told CNBC it has since stopped shipping H200s to China.

Source: Committee summary (PDF) · Licence table (PDF)

Details

Confirmed (committee summary and Commerce licence-status table, both dated Nov 19; the table is headed “as of May 11, 2026”). Chairman Mast released the data under the Export Control Reform Act (Mast release); ranking member Meeks said the disclosure was approved by unanimous consent (Meeks remarks).

The approvals are for H200 (Nvidia) and MI325X (AMD) chips across 21 applications, including 75,000 for Alibaba, 150,000 for Tencent and its subsidiaries, 43,000 for Baidu and 4,000 for BYD, all on the Department of War’s June 2026 list of Chinese military companies. Alibaba and subsidiaries asked for 948,950. The committee also holds applications not fully reflected in the May 11 summary, including Nvidia’s Dec 19, 2025 application for 445,000 H200s for Tencent. The “low” diversion-risk wording is what each Nvidia and AMD application stated, not a Commerce rating.

Nvidia’s John Rizzo told CNBC: “Despite receiving some licenses, we have since stopped shipping H200s to China.” AMD said it complies with US export-control law; Commerce said licensing decisions are “the product of deliberation and consultation among multiple U.S. Government agencies” (CNBC).

Co‑lead · California · Nov 19

California’s attorney general subpoenas Nvidia over chips and China

Attorney General Rob Bonta served an investigative subpoena asking whether Nvidia’s chips or confidential information reached China, and whether Nvidia misled investors and the public.

Why it matters: a subpoena demands information; it is not a charge or a lawsuit, and its text is not public.

Source: California Department of Justice release

Details

Confirmed (release dated Thursday, Nov 19). It ties the subpoena to news of former CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy. Bonta says the office is investigating whether Nvidia or Huang’s actions “resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information — and if Nvidia misled investors and the public about this risk, in violation of the law.” The release has no Nvidia response; CNBC said Nvidia did not immediately respond.

Also · US Congress · Nov 19

Three House members publish an outline for a federal AI safety law

Reps. Jacobs, Maloy and Beyer released an outline of the forthcoming SAFE AI Act, which would require frontier developers to publish safety frameworks, report incidents and pass independent pre-deployment evaluations.

Why it matters: it is an outline, not bill text: no bill number, penalty amounts or coverage thresholds yet.

Source: Outline (PDF) · Jacobs release

Details

Confirmed (outline PDF and Jacobs release, Nov 19). The outline would also require developers to disclose substantially autonomous AI research, set civil penalties and narrow emergency mitigation authority subject to judicial review, and let states go further than the federal floor. Safety frameworks “cannot be unilaterally loosened”; CAISI (the Center for AI Standards and Innovation) would write minimum requirements and assign evaluators, and “Developers do not pay evaluators directly”; startups and academics “are not generally covered”.

Follow-up · Japan · Nov 18 · Nov 12 brief

Japan’s AI law working group outlines safety and reporting duties

A Cabinet Office working group’s draft outline says the law should impose safety and reporting duties on providers of AI that could cause serious harm, with penalties only for breaking reporting duties or orders.

Why it matters: it is a draft outline, not a bill; foreign services used in Japan to a certain degree would be covered.

Source: Meeting page (Japanese) · Draft outline (Japanese, PDF)

Details

Confirmed (meeting page and four-page draft outline; third meeting, held by circulation on Nov 18, 2026). Government recommendation and order powers would back the duties. The outline says regulating AI development or use as such is “not appropriate”, and that penalties should be indirect (for breaching reporting duties or orders), not direct penalties on AI research, development or use. Some firms did not cooperate with an Article 16 survey. It sets no numeric capability threshold, penalty amounts or bill timing. The working group’s earlier draft direction was in the Nov 12 brief.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one or more outlets’ reporting, not officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges or too close to separate.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
