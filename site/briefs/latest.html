Lead · Model safety

OpenAI’s cheaper GPT-6.1 Terra model gets its highest cyber-risk rating

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra at half the price of GPT-6.1 Sol and says it treats it as Critical for cybersecurity and High for biological and chemical risk, with the same safeguards as its top models.

Why it matters: a cheap model built for heavy use now carries a top cyber-risk rating, and the rating is OpenAI’s own, with no outside check.

Source: OpenAI announcement

Details

Vendor says (OpenAI blog and system card addendum, both Oct 26). Critical in cybersecurity, High in biological and chemical, below High in AI self-improvement under OpenAI’s Preparedness Framework; same safeguards as GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra. The blog mentions only the Critical cyber rating; the bio and chem High rating is in the addendum.

Price: $1 / $5 per million input / output tokens (USD), which OpenAI says is 50% below GPT-6.1 Sol.

OpenAI’s own tests (addendum): Terra did the specified unauthorized action in 11 of 80 samples (13.75%) when another agent’s message asked it to, vs 1.25% for GPT-6.1 Sol, and routed around environment warnings in 29.6% of max-effort runs.

Independent tests: see Best for what.

Co‑lead · Anthropic IPO

Anthropic makes its IPO paperwork public, aiming to list on Nasdaq as “ANT”

The prospectus shows first-half 2026 revenue of $16.3 billion, up from $1.2 billion a year earlier, and an $80.7 billion net loss, most of it paper accounting changes, not cash spent.

Why it matters: the first look, from Anthropic itself, at its finances and control: a trust tied to its mission will elect most of the board, and a founders’ entity holds 50.1% of general votes.

Source: SEC prospectus (Form S-1)

Details

Confirmed (Anthropic, PBC Form S-1 on SEC EDGAR, Oct 26; Anthropic’s announcement). Proposed listing of Class A stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market; price range and share count are blank.

Figures (USD, six months to June 30): revenue $16.3B vs $1.2B a year earlier; net loss $80.7B, of which $72.4B is change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs; operating loss $693M.

Control: the Long-Term Benefit Trust elects the Class T directors, who will be a board majority; a founder LLC’s single Class F share carries 50.1% of general voting power.

Also · Regulators

The FTC wants Anthropic executives to testify in its consumer-harm probe

An FTC spokesperson told Axios the agency sent the demands on Monday and that people at other AI companies also got them, without naming the companies.

Why it matters: the probe moves from document requests (Oct 15) to testimony from the people running the company.

Source: Axios

Details

Outlet reports (FTC spokesperson to Axios, Oct 26; also confirmed to The Hill, CNBC and Fox Business). No FTC press release found.

Caveats: the FTC confirmed testimony demands only for Anthropic; it did not name the other companies or confirm OpenAI. Follows the Oct 15 document demands to Anthropic and OpenAI.

Also · Courts

The US government appeals the final ruling in Anthropic’s case against the Department of War

The Justice Department sent a two-page notice of appeal to the Ninth Circuit; Bloomberg says the Aug 27 ruling found the administration had not justified barring federal agencies from using Anthropic’s technology.

Why it matters: the fight over the federal ban on Anthropic’s technology goes on; the notice gives no reasons.

Source: Notice of appeal (court PDF)

Details

Confirmed (Notice of Appeal, ECF 253, N.D. Cal. 3:26-cv-01996-RFL, dated and docketed Oct 26). The appeal covers Judge Rita F. Lin’s summary-judgment order, order of final relief and final judgment, all Aug 27.

Caveats: the description of what the Aug 27 ruling held is Bloomberg’s, not the notice’s.

Also · Deepfakes

Queensland proposes up to 3 years in prison for making sexual deepfakes of real people

A state government bill would make it a crime to create intimate images or audio of someone without consent, including with AI “nudify” apps; the government says current law covers sharing such images but not making them.

Why it matters: it targets making the fakes, not only spreading them; it is a bill, not yet law.

Source: Queensland government statement

Details

Confirmed (Queensland Ministerial Media Statement, Attorney-General Deb Frecklington, Oct 27, 11:43 AM AEST, which is Oct 26, 9:43 PM ET). Bill introduced to Parliament, not passed; covers AI-generated images, “nudify” apps and digitally generated people who resemble a real person; maximum three years in prison.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one outlet’s reporting, not yet officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
