Lead · UK

UK promises money for an AI biosecurity lab and more for its AI Security Institute

The Treasury announced a national AI biosecurity plan, including a lab that OpenAI and Anthropic will back with free use of their AI models, plus extra funding for the AI Security Institute.

Why it matters: a government is paying for AI safety testing and bio-defence, with two top labs chipping in; the money is promised in next week’s Budget, not yet spent.

Source: HM Treasury release

Details

Confirmed (HM Treasury, Oct 24): £165m National AI Biosecurity Initiative (£100m for the AI Biosecurity Lab, £65m for bio-surveillance and countermeasures); an “additional £120 million” for AISI; £60m for an agentic-AI National Cyber Shield; £70m for a National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability (NERV) Centre; £10m a year to study how AI will reshape jobs.

Lab credits: per the Cabinet Office page, OpenAI will provide £100m in compute and model credits and Anthropic “up to £100 million”; the lab will be an independent non-profit.

Caveat: commitments to be set out in the Budget, not yet spent. The “£600m+” total is techUK’s figure quoted in the release, not the government’s.

Co‑lead · Texas

Texas sues OpenAI and Sam Altman, asking a court to keep ChatGPT from Texas kids

Attorney General Ken Paxton’s lawsuit says OpenAI misled users and harmed Texans; it also asks that an outside safety reviewer picked by the court approve any model release to Texas users.

Why it matters: a state is asking a judge to pre-clear AI model releases; these are allegations, with no ruling yet.

Source: Texas AG release

Details

Confirmed that the suit exists (Texas AG, Oct 23). The press release cites only the Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA); the petition (PDF) also pleads the SCOPE Act, a Texas children’s online-safety law.

Petition asks for injunctions including against “Offering ChatGPT to minors in Texas” and “Releasing any new artificial intelligence model to Texas users unless an independent third-party evaluator, approved by the Court, has reviewed its safety guardrails and approved its release”, plus civil penalties of $10,000 per DTPA violation; it seeks “monetary relief over $1,000,000”.

OpenAI told the Texas Tribune it is “committed to working with Texas and other states”. Separate from the eight-state suit in an earlier brief.

Also · China

China asks for job-impact checks on big AI projects

A State Council policy paper says AI projects that could cause major job losses should get a check on how many jobs they could cost before approval.

Why it matters: China’s cabinet is tying AI rollout to jobs in a formal policy paper; most of its 18 measures are encouragements.

Source: gov.cn (Chinese)

Details

Confirmed (Guobanfa [2026] No. 29, dated Oct 16, published Oct 23). The firm step: employment risk assessments during review for AI projects in market-access fields that may cause a major employment impact. AI job displacement joins the large-scale unemployment response mechanism.

Caveat: unemployment buffer periods with advance notice to workers are only “encouraged”. Published two days ago.

Also · Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania now requires AI labels on misleading ads

A law signed Oct 22 requires a clear AI disclosure on ads whose synthetic content gives a false impression of a product; it takes effect in 60 days.

Why it matters: one more US state is setting AI-disclosure rules for advertisers, enforced by the attorney general and district attorneys.

Source: PA General Assembly, SB 806

Details

Confirmed (Act 99 of 2026, approved Oct 22; Senate 48-2, House 163-40). Covers ads that use synthetic content “in a manner that creates a false consumer perception about the nature, origin or authenticity” of goods or services. Exceptions: “evidently fictional” content, parody, satire, commentary or criticism, and content “limited to text”. Civil fines up to $3,000, per the enacted text.

Caveat: The Verge says text ads are covered, which conflicts with the bill’s text-only exception; we go with the bill text.

Also · Elections

Study: two chatbots lean opposite ways when asked to pick midterm candidates

An audit asked GPT-6.1 Sol and Grok 4.7, through the versions developers use, with web search turned on, to name one candidate in each 2026 race.

Why it matters: GPT-6.1 Sol mostly picked Democrats and Grok 4.7 mostly Republicans, but the study isn’t peer-reviewed and ChatGPT itself declines such requests.

Source: Convovo AI Ballot 2026

Details

Authors say: Convovo’s data file (data.json) has 506 races with 3 draws each; GPT-6.1 Sol’s verdict was the Democrat in 451 and Grok 4.7’s the Republican in 408 (both run Oct 21). TechCrunch reports 97% and 92% of answers and agreement in under 10% of races.

Caveat: not peer-reviewed; no significance tests published. OpenAI told TechCrunch the API test “doesn’t reflect the experience people have” in ChatGPT; SpaceXAI did not respond.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one outlet’s reporting, not yet officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
