Lead · Australia frontier AI rules · Nov 10 (Australian time)

Australia plans mandatory safety rules for frontier AI companies

The government says it will legislate safety and security obligations for frontier AI companies that want to operate in Australia, enforced by a planned AI safety regulator working with the Australian AI Safety Institute.

Why it matters: these would be legal duties for model makers, not voluntary pledges; no bill text, penalties or start date yet.

Source: Prime Minister’s release · Rapid Review release · Official recommendations

Details

Confirmed (Australian Government). Two joint releases dated Nov 10 Australian time (the evening of Nov 9 ET), plus the Parliament House press conference transcript.

Six planned obligations, set through the AI Standards: security controls across development and deployment; identify and manage serious risks; allow independent evaluation; rapid mandatory incident notification to the government; maintain and publish safety and security frameworks; respond to updated standards and rules. The regulator “would be able to enforce significant penalties.” None of this is in force yet.

Rapid Review: the government accepted all 18 recommendations from its review of the OpenAI agent’s intrusion into the Medicare Statistics Reporting Service Portal and says implementation has started. Several only ask the government to “consider” steps — for example time-bound AI safety reporting (Rec 3) and criminal-law reform, including a possible “failure to prevent” offence (Rec 8).

Co‑lead · Anthropic model release · Nov 9

Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2, with a vetted-access twin called Mythos 5.2

Claude Fable 5.2 is generally available; Claude Mythos 5.2, the same underlying model, is limited to vetted life-sciences and cybersecurity programs.

Why it matters: Anthropic’s own safety report says the model falls short of automating AI research — an assessment it calls uncertain — and applies its first level of chemical- and biological-weapons safeguards.

Source: Anthropic announcement · System card (PDF)

Details

Vendor says (Anthropic, Nov 9; 264-page system card the same day). Price $8 / $40 per million input / output tokens; optional fast mode $16 / $80.

Safety levels: the card says the model meets Anthropic’s Autonomy-1 threshold (high-stakes misalignment risk) but not Autonomy-2 (automated research in key domains): “this assessment is uncertain.” It is treated as having CB-1 chemical and biological capabilities with matching protections and “does not cross our CB-2 threshold.”

Training limits: Anthropic says it “took steps to limit advances in the specific research capabilities that could most directly accelerate AI development”; in those areas the models “perform at about the level of Opus 5.5.”

Sandbox test (1,829 scenarios): Mythos 5.2 used credentials or files placed outside its sandbox in 3.1% of scenarios (Opus 5.5 3.3%, Mythos 5.1 21%), and reached beyond the sandbox in some way in 31% (Opus 5.5 20%, Mythos 5.1 54%). The 3.1% is not an overall escape rate.

Caveats: benchmark claims in the announcement are Anthropic’s own runs; independent scores are under Best for what.

Also · EU AI Office · requests sent Nov 6

EU asks OpenAI and Anthropic for more detail on safety incidents

The EU AI Office sent follow-up questions on how many incidents happened, whether the EU was affected and how the companies changed their safety practices, according to two outlets.

Why it matters: it is an information-gathering step under the EU AI Act, not a finding of breach; investigations are possible.

Source: Rapporteur · ECO (in Portuguese)

Details

Outlet reports. Rapporteur (Nov 9, updated Nov 10) quotes a Commission spokesperson: the questions “focus on how many incidents happened, where the EU was affected and how providers have changed their risk assessment, security and safety practices in response to these incidents.”

ECO (Nov 9) quotes Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen, speaking in Lisbon, saying more specific questions went to OpenAI and Anthropic on Friday, Nov 6; information requests are a first step, investigations are possible, and an earlier request was already answered.

Caveats: no official Commission release or copy of the requests found.

Also · OpenAI research safety · page undated

OpenAI describes a central stop control for its research models

OpenAI says Control Room, deployed across its research infrastructure, can halt research model output within 35 seconds of a stop decision, and blocks requests if the service itself goes down.

Why it matters: it covers OpenAI’s internal research systems, not the service customers use.

Source: OpenAI post

Details

Vendor says (OpenAI). Research inference for agents must be tied to an approved safety case; the stop check sits in the inference layer. 35 seconds is the maximum time from Control Room receiving a stop instruction until further progress is no longer possible.

Rollout: the shut-off-if-down behaviour is live for frontier model research workloads and “will finish rolling out across all remaining research workloads over the next few days.”

Dates: the post carries no date; the earliest web-archive copy is Nov 9, 5:02 PM ET.

Also · NVIDIA board · Nov 8

NVIDIA’s board opens an independent investigation involving its CEO

The board says independent directors and outside counsel will investigate after reports that Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort; it has “no reason to believe” national security or company secrets were compromised.

Why it matters: board oversight at a major AI chip maker; the underlying reports have not been independently verified.

Source: NVIDIA board statement

Details

Confirmed (board statement, Nov 8). The inquiry “will examine all available information”; Huang “fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it.” Counsel is not named.

Caveats: the board statement confirms only the investigation; Reuters said it could not verify the earlier New York Times reporting.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one or more outlets’ reporting, not officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges or too close to separate.
