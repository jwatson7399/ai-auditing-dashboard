Lead · US Commerce Department · Nov 23

US adds seven companies in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand to its export restriction list

The Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) added seven entities to its Entity List, citing a “significant risk” that they divert “advanced computing commodities” to unauthorized end uses or end users.

Why it matters: a licence is now required for every item subject to US export rules, and licence requests are presumed denied. BIS posted the rule for public inspection on Nov 23 (4:15 p.m.); formal publication is scheduled for Nov 25.

Source: BIS final rule, public inspection · Rule text (PDF)

Details

Confirmed (final rule, Federal Register document 2026-23999, opened on public inspection). The rule says it is effective on the date of public inspection and that the additions are made “because of the significant risk of these entities being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or end users.”

  • Malaysia (3): MSFusion Sdn. Bhd.; PT Megaspeed International; Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd.
  • Singapore (3): Apex Enterprise Solutions (Singapore) Private Limited; Megaspeed International Pte. Ltd. (also listed as 7Road International); Swiftdata Pte. Ltd.
  • Thailand (1): Megaspeed (Thailand) Co., Ltd. (also listed as 7Road International).

The rule lists Megaspeed International and Swiftdata as separate entries and does not call one the parent of the other; any parent link is outlet reporting, not in the rule. Shipments already en route may proceed if completed within 30 days of public inspection. Related: the Nov 22 brief covered the congressional letter on the Megaspeed probe.

Co‑lead · United Nations and Barbados · Nov 23

UN-backed AI safety summit asks governments to develop a global framework within a year

The Bridgetown summit, co-hosted by the UN and Barbados, issued a two-page Call to Action asking all Member States to develop “within the next year” a “globally inclusive and principles-based Multilateral Framework for AI risk management, backed by independent and credible oversight.”

Why it matters: it is a non-binding call, not an agreed treaty, and it names no signatory states. Among the appeals it lists are liability, insurance, incident reporting and testing in local languages.

Source: Call to Action (PDF, dated Nov 23) · UN Secretary-General’s remarks, Nov 23

Details

Confirmed (the two-page Call to Action, dated 23 November 2026, and the Secretary-General’s remarks of the same day). The Call also urges donors “to increase investments in AI capacity-building” in the Global South, as “an early commitment to the Global Fund for AI Capacity-Building proposed to the UN General Assembly,” and lists appeals “we heard” including “kill switches from point of purchase” in procurement.

Secretary-General António Guterres: “Put simply, we need a common foundation for AI safety.” UN News paraphrases the ask as a call “to begin discussions”; the Call and his remarks use the stronger “develop within the next year.” UN News also notes he leaves office at the end of December.

How we rank
  • Ties: on boards with error ranges, rank = 1 + models whose whole range sits above (LMArena’s rule), so overlaps show “=”. Boards with no ranges are ranked by value only and called “close race” when the gap is small.
  • Tags: Confirmed = official or independent source · Vendor says = the company’s or authors’ own account · Outlet reports = one or more outlets’ reporting, not officially confirmed · Too close to call = tied within ranges or too close to separate.
  • Jobs: each pick keeps its own as-of date and is carried forward until its board updates. Independent boards first (AA = Artificial Analysis, Vals, LMArena, Epoch, Scale, ARC Prize). Harnesses, scales and method versions are never mixed.
