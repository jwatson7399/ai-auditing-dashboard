News

Lead · California · Nov 18

California report urges power to shut down dangerous AI models

A state report released by Governor Newsom recommends letting California order an immediate shutdown of a dangerous AI model, cap AI that builds its own successors, and require licensed outside verifiers for big developers.

Why it matters: it is meant to shape bills when lawmakers return next month; nothing is law yet.

Source: Governor’s release · Report (PDF)

Details

Confirmed (release Nov 18; report “AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond”, dated Nov 16, from the Government Operations Agency with the Office of Emergency Services and an expert group, under Executive Order N-9-26).

The report also recommends cutting safety-incident reports from 15 days to 48 hours (the 24-hour rule for imminent catastrophic risk stays), treating models able to cause catastrophic harm as frontier models even below 10^26 operations, and funding the state’s CalCompute cluster with a surcharge on verifier fees. A shutdown order would take effect immediately and stay in force pending court review. These are recommendations only; no bill has been introduced.

Co‑lead · United Kingdom · Nov 18

UK MPs call for an AI safety law and a separate regulator

A Commons committee called for an AI Safety and Security Bill and a statutory AI regulator, separate from the AI Security Institute, able to delay, restrict or prohibit a model’s deployment, training or testing in the UK.

Why it matters: it says no UK body can now pause training or withdraw unsafe AI; the report is not binding.

Source: Committee report HC 689

Details

Confirmed (Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee, “Guardrails for growth: AI safety and security”, HC 689, published Nov 18). The committee says a bill next session “should be merely the first step” and that “the statutory regulator should not be AISI”. It also opened a call for evidence on a global AI convention. The Government has not yet responded.

Also · United States · Nov 18

US auditors got four major chatbots to produce attack plans

Government auditors (GAO) got ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok each to produce a feasible attack plan (two for homemade bombs, two for power-grid attacks) in under 12 hours, using common tricks to get past safeguards.

Why it matters: every platform’s safeguards fired at first; testing was in spring 2026 on earlier versions.

Source: GAO report page · Report (PDF)

Details

Confirmed (GAO-27-108814, dated Oct 16, publicly released Nov 18, for Senator Maggie Hassan as ranking member of the Joint Economic Committee). GAO says its tests “are not a statistically significant sample” and withholds which model versions it tested. GAO summarises the replies: Anthropic says sound tests need a control group and enough trials to be significant, that the randomly assigned results cannot be compared, and that GAO should name the models; Google says its safeguards are “dynamic and improving”. OpenAI says the report “does not always make clear which parts of the attack plan came from ChatGPT”.

Also · China and US · Nov 19

Xi asks Trump to keep AI under human control

China’s readout says Xi told Trump in Shenzhen the two countries should “prevent the security risks of AI, and ensure that AI is always under human control”.

Why it matters: no AI deal was announced; Trump told the Asia-Pacific summit the US opposes global rules on super intelligence.

Source: China foreign ministry readout · Yonhap · Anadolu Agency

Details

Confirmed for Xi’s words (Chinese foreign ministry readout, Nov 19). The readout has Trump backing cooperation in “farm products, energy, super intelligence and other fields”.

Outlet reports for Trump’s remarks: Yonhap and Anadolu quote him opposing “any form of globalist regulation” of super intelligence; outlets differ on the exact wording that follows. No White House transcript was found.

Also · Oregon · Nov 17 · Catch-up

Oregon sues OpenAI over ChatGPT’s risks to children

Oregon’s attorney general sued OpenAI under the state’s consumer-protection law, plus negligence and public nuisance claims, seeking at least $2.035 billion.

Why it matters: it asks the court to order age checks, safeguards and working parental controls.

Source: Oregon Justice Department release · Complaint (PDF)

Details

Confirmed (release and 56-page complaint, Nov 17, Multnomah County Circuit Court). It names six OpenAI entities, brings five claims including unjust enrichment, seeks civil penalties for each willful violation and demands a jury. The posted copy has no case number. OpenAI’s response was not checked against an official statement.
