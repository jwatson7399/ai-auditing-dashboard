Held / revisit (compact)

What’s new on the boards

New independent readings today: Anthropic’s Claude Haiku 5.5, plus a new Scale AI board. Anthropic released Haiku 5.5 on Oct 7. It’s today’s newly audited model, so it’s the only one with the blue accent. On Artificial Analysis (AA) it scores 43 on the Intelligence Index (13 behind Claude Sonnet 5.5 at 56). On Vals AI it gets 90.44% on Vibe Code Bench (#3 of 110, statistically tied with the top two) and 54.31% ±1.41 on the Vals Index (#16 of 45). Terminal-Bench 4.0 depends on who runs it: AA 33%, Vals 35.35% and Anthropic’s own 39.2%, three setups that aren’t comparable. New board: Scale AI’s Humanity’s Sixth Sense (HSS) tests intuitive visual reasoning on images and video; GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol share rank 1, far below the 93.1% human baseline. Also new: Mistral Large 4 (preview) joined LMArena’s WebDev board at 1534 ±21 (#45 of 141, with few votes), and the WebDev card now uses LMArena’s Oct 7 board. Nine cards gain rows or a refresh; every other job card is carried and shows its own as-of date.

Epoch method notes (no card): Epoch’s new InnovationEval asks whether a sandboxed agent can rediscover a recent machine-learning training method (SDPO, on-policy self-distillation, a way of further training a model on its own outputs) without having seen it. In early single runs, GPT-5.6 Sol’s in-scope method got 15% of SDPO’s gains (35% on a generous reading of scope), and two newer agents had memorized the paper, so it isn’t a ranking. Separately, Epoch banned one in-game card from the default setup of EBR-bench (Epoch’s long-horizon test in which models play the board game Earthborne Rangers) after GPT-6 Astra used it to reach 100%. Under the ban Astra’s best is 20 of 21 objectives, and older EBR-bench results, an input to the Epoch Capabilities Index, aren’t directly comparable.

Independent, beyond the cards below: on AA-Omniscience (AA’s factual-knowledge test, which also tracks how often a model answers wrongly instead of admitting it doesn’t know) its accuracy is 36%, against 55% for Gemini 3.8 Flash and 44% for GPT-6 Luna, but its hallucination rate is lower (40% vs 55% and 77%); AA puts part of the gap down to “more willingness to admit when it doesn’t know.” It is token-hungry: about 162k output tokens per Index task, roughly 3× GPT-6 Luna (max). Going from Extra-high to Max effort adds 2 Index points for about 1.8× the tokens, and at High effort it scores 38. As of Oct 7, AA measured 243.4 output tokens per second on Anthropic’s API (#9 of 182). On Vals it costs $2.99 per Index test and used the most output tokens of any model in the Vals Index top 28; Vals logs a 0.22% refusal rate.

Vendor-reported by Anthropic (harness and effort not stated, not ranked): Terminal-Bench 4.0 39.2%, OSWorld 2.1 72.4% (offline subset only) and Humanity’s Last Exam 45.9% without tools (57.4% with tools). AA’s own Humanity’s Last Exam run, part of its Index, gives 44.4%. Anthropic’s GDPval-AA figure (1620) matches AA’s model page.

Caveats: AA’s $0.21 per Index task is provisional because AA’s site “does not yet reflect tiered pricing” (the higher price above 100k-token prompts). AA says its AutomationBench-AA result (35%) is “likely understated” because a pre-release safety issue made the model over-refuse; AA will re-run it and expects it to rise, which could lift the Index. The three Terminal-Bench 4.0 figures come from three different harnesses, so never average them.

The job

Hand an agent a one-page, plain-English description of a web app, let it build the whole thing in a full development environment, then have an automated tester click through the result to check each feature works.

What changed

A model priced at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens now sits in the top group for “describe it, get a working app.” It burns a lot of tokens to get there, so check total cost per job rather than list price. On AA’s terminal test it’s far behind the frontier, so it’s a candidate for app scaffolding and prototypes, not a drop-in for long shell-heavy agent runs. Try it on your own specs before switching.

Terms used below: AA = Artificial Analysis, an independent testing firm; Agent NI = Net Improvement, LMArena Agent Arena’s score for how much better real agent sessions go with a model; Elo = a head-to-head rating built from people’s votes (higher wins more often); WER = word error rate (lower is better); ECI = Epoch Capabilities Index, a composite capability scale (not a percent); pass@1 = solved on a single attempt; HSS = Scale AI’s Humanity’s Sixth Sense. Blue accent: Claude Haiku 5.5 only, as today’s newly audited model.

Where they land

    Don’t mix harnesses: Agent Arena Net Improvement ≠ Elo and ≠ pass-rate agent benches. LMArena Text/Vision Elo ≠ accuracy %. LMArena WebDev Elo ≠ Vibe Code Bench. AA Terminal-Bench 4.0 ≠ Vals TB 4.0 mini-swe-agent ≠ Anthropic vendor TB 4.0. Streaming WER is lower-better and not an LLM score. Epoch ECI ≠ AA Intelligence Index: different scales, don’t blend. Scale HSS pass rate ≠ LMArena Vision Elo. AA-Omniscience accuracy ≠ the AA-Omniscience Index. CyberGym-E2E-AA is one of three parts of the AA Cyber Index, so don’t read them as two separate wins. LMArena image Elo ≠ Google’s internal model-card Elo.

    Caveats

    Cantwell framework: principles, not a bill. There is no legislative text, no definition of “covered models,” no threshold and no penalties. The liability plank keeps existing civil and criminal liability; it doesn’t create a new right to sue.

    GPT-6 safety figures are OpenAI’s own. The under-18 and production-benchmark regressions come from OpenAI’s system card and haven’t been checked independently. The 44% faster web-search answers figure is an OpenAI internal evaluation. OpenAI’s Help Center still names GPT-5.6 Luna for Free and Go, which conflicts with the post.

    Australia: an approach, not a design. The end-of-year timing for standards and 2027 legislation is ABC’s reporting, not the minister’s speech. “Hacked” and “breached” are the minister’s words.

    CLAIM Act: discussion draft. No bill number; we couldn’t check Congress.gov. Its reading as near-strict liability is ours, based on the “regardless of the degree of care exercised” text.

    Claude Haiku 5.5. Anthropic’s benchmark table is self-reported. AA’s cost per task is provisional, and AA says its AutomationBench-AA result is likely understated pending a re-run. AA, Vals and Anthropic report three different Terminal-Bench 4.0 figures from three harnesses; don’t compare or average them. Anthropic’s table shows 16.4% for GPT-6 Luna on Terminal-Bench 4.0, where AA shows 13%.

    Scale HSS. Answers are graded by an AI judge (Claude-Opus-5, an Anthropic model), which can favour its own family; Scale’s cross-judge check on five models limits but doesn’t rule that out. Scale’s page says every model ran at high effort, while its table labels entries max or xhigh. Test media come from the public web, so models may have seen some of it in training.

    Correction (Vals Index). Our Oct 7 brief labelled Mistral Large 4’s Vals result “Vals Index v3” and said it wasn’t comparable with our Vals Index card. Vals’ page lists the current version as v2.1 (released Sep 25, with Terminal-Bench 4.0 in the coding bucket), and it’s one board, so today the two cards are merged.

    Epoch notes. InnovationEval results are single runs on one task, two of them contaminated by memorization, so they aren’t a ranking. After the EBR-bench card ban, older EBR-bench results aren’t directly comparable with new ones, and that test feeds the carried Epoch Capabilities Index card.

    Held: SynthID Detector reads SynthID watermarks only, so a negative result doesn’t mean content is human-made; usage figures are Google’s. Mistral Large 4’s weights and licence still aren’t published.

    Carried scores. Apart from the cards marked NEW or UPDATED, the job cards and the CE board show the scores from the Oct 7 brief (see each card’s as-of date). On every card with confidence intervals (ECI, Agent NI, Text, Vision, WebDev, Text-to-Image, Image Edit, HSS), ranks count only models whose interval sits entirely above. Overlap alone never pushes a model down (LMArena’s method), but two overlapping models can still get different ranks when a third model clears one of them and not the other. Rows stay in score order, so ranks can skip or sit out of sequence. Agent Arena Net Improvement ≠ Elo; Text, Vision and image boards are preference Elo, not accuracy; AA Output Speed is speed, not quality. Prefer AA / Vals / LMArena / Epoch / Scale for decisions, and don’t blend harnesses. Scores within about 3 points are within noise on most % boards; intervals matter on ECI, HSS and LMArena.
