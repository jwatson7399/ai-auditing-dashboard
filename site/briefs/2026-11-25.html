Lead · New York State · Nov 24

New York bans AI chatbot toys for five years, starting Feb 22, 2027

Gov. Kathy Hochul signed S.9408-A/A.11144-B (Chapter 416), which bars making, distributing or selling “chatbot toys”: children’s toys with a built-in AI companion.

Why it matters: it starts on Feb 22, 2027 (the 90th day after signing), so it does not cover this holiday season; the attorney general can seek up to $15,000 per day for each violation.

Source: Governor’s release, Nov 24 · Rush transcript · NY Assembly bill page, S.9408

Details

Confirmed (Governor’s release and rush transcript, Nov 24; the Assembly bill page lists it signed as Chapter 416 with approval memo 14, whose text is not posted yet). The ban covers manufacturing, exchanging, holding for sale, distributing or selling a chatbot toy, and is repealed five years after it takes effect.

The law defines a chatbot toy by combining the state’s “AI companion” definition with the federal children’s-toy definition; the release’s “internet-connected” wording is not in the law. The ban applies to “any person,” but only the attorney general can sue, and the lawsuit runs against an “operator”; no proof of injury is needed. The Assembly passed it 140–0 on June 2. It is separate from the companion-chatbot law in the Oct 29 brief (S.9051-B, Chapter 368); both add an Article 48 to the state’s business law, and how that numbering is reconciled is not yet clear.

Co‑lead · State attorneys general · Nov 24

42 attorneys general ask a court to block Character.AI’s sale of users’ chats

In Character.AI’s bankruptcy case, the states object to selling its platform and nearly all its data, including chats from more than 21 million US accounts (over 2.1 million registered to minors), to Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC for $10 million plus assumed liabilities.

Why it matters: a court hearing on the sale is set for Dec 3–4; the states want chats left out unless users agree after direct notice, and child-safety measures kept.

Source: California Attorney General’s release, Nov 24 · States’ objection (PDF, California copy) · Same objection (PDF, Oregon copy)

Details

Confirmed (California Attorney General’s release, Nov 24; the states’ objection in In re Character Technologies, Inc., Case 26-11603-BLS, Doc 219, docketed Nov 20). The caption and the release’s list name 42 attorneys general, including DC; Attorney General Bonta’s quote says 45. A Dec 1 auction date appears in the release, not in the objection.

Per the objection, Smirks was formed Nov 13 and chosen as the opening (“stalking horse”) bidder Nov 14, with a $350,000 break-up fee and a $1 million deposit. The states also ask that any buyer honour existing privacy promises, follow state law and not pass the data on without notice and approval. Texas objected separately; the coalition partly joins it. Character.AI’s earlier Disney deal was in the Oct 19 brief.
