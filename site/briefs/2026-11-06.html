Lead · US AI enforcement

The Justice Department stands up a Super Intelligence Task Force

The Justice Department announced an internal Super Intelligence Task Force to coordinate criminal and civil enforcement, prosecution guidance and compliance assessment, absorbing its earlier AI Task Force.

Why it matters: labs and buyers get a single Justice Department contact for super-intelligence cases; the memo creates no enforceable rights.

Source: Justice Department release · Memorandum (PDF)

Details

Confirmed (Justice Department). Press release dated Nov 5; the linked memorandum header is dated Oct 13, so when the body was established is unresolved.

Role: co-chaired by Associate AG Stanley Woodward and Associate DAG Stephen Weiss; absorbs the AI Task Force announced Jan 9, 2026. It will organize criminal and civil super-intelligence enforcement, deconflict investigations, recommend priorities, develop prosecution guidance, advise on legislation, and serve as contact with the broader Super Intelligence Force.

Duties for components: notify the task force when opening super-intelligence investigations and before charges or resolutions, except in urgent cases.

Caveats: an internal management measure; the memo says it creates no enforceable rights or benefits. Distinct from the Pentagon technology chief’s earlier promise (Nov 4) that a government task force would list laws that apply to AI labs.

Co‑lead · AI R&D speed

Outside reviewers say Anthropic’s well-specified-task progress sped up about 1.9×

METR estimates Anthropic’s scores on its well-specified-task benchmark grew at about 1.9 times the pre-October 2025 rate, and judges that AI use in research likely explains most of the current speed-up, with wide uncertainty.

Why it matters: it is an outside check on Anthropic’s own “accelerated progress” talk, not proof that a formal safety threshold was crossed.

Source: METR assessment

Details

Confirmed (METR). Assessment dated Nov 5; high-access review Aug 12–Oct 29, 2026.

Figures: estimated 1.9× the pre-October 2025 growth rate on AECI, 95% CI 1.5–2.8×. METR is confident the speed-up was not from faster training-compute scaling. Its subjective guess puts roughly four-fifths of the October 2026 speed-up on AI use in R&D, with substantial uncertainty; other methods it tried span about 1.1–2.9×.

Caveats: Anthropic redacted most of the evidence METR found most informative; the ~100-page full report is not public. METR did not identify clear bottlenecks that would stop further compounding over the next 12 months. Adds an outside assessment beside Anthropic’s earlier S-1 language; it does not establish that an RSP threshold was crossed.

Also · Cyber model

OpenAI rates GPT-6 Cyber “Critical” for cybersecurity and limits early access

OpenAI says GPT-6 Cyber is Critical under its Preparedness Framework for cybersecurity, matching Astra and above GPT-5.6 Cyber’s High rating, and reports ExploitGym at 48.5% versus 32.1%, with early access limited to approved Daybreak Red customers.

Why it matters: defenders get a stronger cyber agent under tighter access; the capability claims are the company’s own.

Source: OpenAI’s blog post

Details

Vendor says (OpenAI). Post dated Nov 5; figures are OpenAI’s.

Claims: ExploitGym Honeypot 48.5% versus 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber; out-of-scope exploitation avoided in 99.93% of tests without a deployment prompt and 100% with it (versus 94.49% for GPT-5.6 Cyber without prompts). The post attributes 81 high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities (Mozilla’s classification) and the 948 Linux local-privilege-escalation exploits described earlier to work with this model.

Access: Daybreak Red for Critical Infrastructure for approved critical-infrastructure defenders.

Caveats: company figures; external tests summarized in the system card show mixed gains versus Astra. The 948 Linux count repeats an earlier held item and has no outside confirmation.

Also · Data centres

Google plans a $17 billion Wyoming campus and says it will cover its power costs

Google announced a first data centre in Laramie County, Wyoming, at US$17 billion, with a contracted 702 MW natural-gas fuel-cell project plus carbon capture, and said it will pay for the electricity it uses and cover growth-driven infrastructure costs.

Why it matters: a large campus pledge that puts cost and local-grid questions on the table; outcomes are not yet measured.

Source: Google’s blog post

Details

Vendor says (Google). Post by Steve Walter, Nov 5. Partners named: Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy, AEP, Related Digital; fuel cells by Bloom Energy.

Pledges: Google says it will pay for 100% of the power it uses and cover infrastructure needs driven by its growth; the facility will be air-cooled. It describes the 702 MW fuel-cell project with carbon capture as bringing its total contracted carbon-capture capacity to nearly 1 gigawatt. A Community Council with partners is planned for Cheyenne.

Caveats: planned infrastructure and voluntary commitments, not verified emissions outcomes; additional gas generation is involved.

Also · Enterprise controls

Anthropic opens Claude Enterprise inference hooks to all customers

Anthropic made prompt and pre-tool-call inference hooks generally available for Claude Enterprise, so customer security tools can allow or deny before a reply or tool runs; a Security Center and local-VM endpoint detection stay in beta.

Why it matters: security teams get a practical gate on prompts and tools; the center is guidance, not a compliance certificate.

Source: Anthropic’s article

Details

Vendor says (Anthropic). Article dated Nov 5.

Generally available: inference hooks that send prompts and tool calls to the customer’s security or DLP tools for allow/deny before inference or tool execution.

Still beta: Security Center, centralized management, Admin API sign-out, self-serve IP allowlist, Desktop settings, and endpoint detection in the local Cowork virtual machine (detection mode only; excludes cloud Cowork sessions).

Caveats: Anthropic says the Security Center compares settings to its recommended configuration and is not a compliance certification.
