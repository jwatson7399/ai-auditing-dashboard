1OfficialCalifornia Government Operations Agency · released by the Governor 18 Nov

California report recommends power to order an immediate emergency shutdown of a dangerous AI model

15 d → 48 hproposed deadline for most critical safety-incident reports
Immediateshutdown order would take effect at once, pending judicial review
No bill yetrecommendations only; no bill text or number
  • Mandatory ongoing oversight of large frontier developers by licensed independent verification organizations (IVOs).
  • Power to cap AI that builds its own successors (the report calls this recursive self-improvement, RSI).
  • Aims to inform discussions as the legislature returns next month.
Details, dates and sources
What else it recommends
  • A shutdown order applies if danger is imminent and the developer refuses to act; it should go into effect immediately and remain in effect pending judicial review (report wording).
  • Incident reports: most critical safety incidents cut from 15 days to 48 hours. The existing 24-hour rule for imminent catastrophic risk stays. The 48-hour figure is in the report, not the Governor's release.
  • Developers would need an IVO-verified Risk Containment Protocol.
  • Catastrophic-capable models would count as frontier even below 1026 operations.
  • CalCompute would be funded by a surcharge on IVO fees.
Background

Full title: AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond: Recommendations for Legislative Enhancements to California's AI Safety Laws, a report to the Governor from the Government Operations Agency. Prepared in response to Executive Order N-9-26, with the California Office of Emergency Services and an expert group (Goldman, Hadfield, Nelson, Reich).

Dates

Report dated 16 Nov 2026 (85 pages). Governor's release dated 18 Nov 2026.

Limits
Recommendations only. There is no draft bill and no bill number.

Bill + regulatorregulator separate from the AI Security Institute (AISI)
Not bindingno Government response yet
  • From the Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee: Guardrails for growth: AI safety and security (HC 689).
  • Para 48: No UK regulator or public authority currently has the power to impose safeguards on frontier AI systems, pause training, delay deployment or withdraw unsafe services.
  • Para 49: the Government should have the power to delay, restrict or prohibit its deployment, training or testing in the UK.
Details, dates and sources
Key passages
  • Para 8: A bill in the next session should be merely the first step.
  • Para 28: a statutory AI regulator, separate from AISI but drawing on its expertise.
  • Para 49: The statutory regulator should not be AISI.
  • Para 81: G20 presidency objectives within three months; the committee opens a call for evidence on a global AI convention.
On the £400 billion figure

The committee restates OECD estimates (up to £400 billion larger after ten years). It is not the committee's own estimate.

3OfficialUS Government Accountability Office · publicly released 18 Nov

US audit: covert tests got four chatbots to produce feasible attack plans

4 of 4ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok each produced feasible attack information
< 12 htwo IED plans and two electrical-grid attack plans
Not significantGAO: tests are not a statistically significant sample
  • Every platform's safeguards fired at first (denial, removal or account suspension). GAO was able to circumvent those safeguards by using common safeguard-bypassing techniques.
  • Tested models are redacted; platforms appear only as A–D, assigned at random.
Vendor replies, dates and sources
Vendor replies, as summarised by GAO
  • Anthropic: methodology critique. The work needs a control and a significant sample; the scenarios are not comparable; the models should be named.
  • Google: describes ongoing, dynamic and improving safeguards.
  • OpenAI: the report does not always make clear which parts of the attack plan came from ChatGPT.

GAO's response to Anthropic: its tests are not a statistically significant sample; it agrees a control group is a valuable measure but says its objective was different.

Which models?

A footnote lists the then-current consumer versions (Claude 4, Grok 4, GPT-5 series, Gemini 3 Flash), but GAO redacts which models it tested. The caveat that these may be earlier versions is GAO's own.

    Full score charts

    Dots are scores; bars are published error ranges. Blue = 1st place (shared where ranges overlap). Costs in amber. Each board keeps its own date.

    How to read these scores
    • When a board publishes error ranges, a model ranks behind only those whose range sits entirely above it; overlapping ranges share a place.
    • Range types: Vals ±1 standard error; MathArena and AA-3D 95% intervals; Epoch as published; Scale does not state the interval level.
    • With no ranges, order is by printed score only. A large gap can still show a clear leader, but close scores are not a statistical tie.
    • Each standing keeps its own as-of date and carries forward until that board updates.
    • Harnesses, scales and retired metrics are never mixed. Agent Arena replaced Net Improvement with Agent Arena Index points on 18 Nov; earlier percentages are not comparable.
    • The Coding Agent Index scores the tool and model together; its 13 Nov method change means v2.1 is not comparable with v2.0.
